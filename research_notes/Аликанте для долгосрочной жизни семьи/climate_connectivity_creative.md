## Q1. Climate normals (AEMET 1991–2020) vs recent summers 2022–2026, records, tropical nights and "noches tórridas"

### Takeaway
Summer 2026 was the hottest at the Alicante observatory since records began in 1869, ahead of 2025, now the second hottest. Summer 2026 also set a record of 15 *noches tórridas* (minimum ≥25 °C). In 2025 the count of tropical nights (minimum ≥20 °C) passed 100 for the first time. The official AEMET 1991–2020 monthly normals could not be read. Only secondary approximations exist: annual mean ≈18 °C, January ≈12 °C, August ≈26.5 °C, and ≈2,700–2,900 sunshine hours a year.

### Cited Findings
**Normals (primary pages located, values not retrievable in this session)**
- AEMET publishes 1991–2020 *valores climatológicos normales* for station 8025 "Alacant/Alicante" (the city observatory, Ciudad Jardín) and station 8019 "Alicante-Elche Aeropuerto". The tables cover T, TM, Tm, R, H, DR, DD (clear days), I (sunshine hours) and more. Sources: [AEMET 8025](https://www.aemet.es/es/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=8025&k=val); [AEMET 8019](https://www.aemet.es/es/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=8019&k=val). The same normals appear in MITECO open data as the "Climatologías normales (1991-2020)" dataset: [datosabiertos.miteco.gob.es](https://catalogo.datosabiertos.miteco.gob.es/catalogo/dataset/2d48e936-95a2-4904-af7e-59d912d002ec).
- Secondary approximations from travel/climate sites, not AEMET tables:
  - Annual mean ≈18 °C, January mean ≈12 °C, August mean ≈26.5 °C.
  - ≈2,900 sunshine hours a year; another site says ">2,700 h".
  - About 6 hours of sun a day in January and 7 in February.
  - Sources: [climasyviajes.com](https://www.climasyviajes.com/clima/espana/alicante); [provinciadealicante.es](https://provinciadealicante.es/clima/); [eltiempo.es](https://www.eltiempo.es/viajar/tiempo-habitual/alicante) *(attribution among these three uncertain)*.
  - The frequently repeated claim of ">300 days of sun a year" is promotional, not an AEMET metric. AEMET counts "días despejados" (clear days), which must be read from the 8025/8019 tables.

**Summer 2026 (June–August)**
- City of Alicante: the hottest summer since at least 1869, when records began. It beat 2025, which is now the second hottest. Sources: [TodoAlicante, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.todoalicante.es/el-tiempo/alicante-registra-verano-caluroso-historia-supera-2025-20260921140411-nt.html); [dsalicante.com, Sep 2026](https://dsalicante.com/noticias/alicante/verano-mas-caluroso-alicante/); [The Leader, 22 Sep 2026](https://theleader.info/2026/09/22/alicante-endures-hottest-summer-in-150-years-as-tropical-nights-hit-record-high/).
- **15 noches tórridas** (minimum ≥25 °C) at the Alicante observatory in summer 2026, an absolute record that beat 2025. Valencia recorded 30 and Castellón 20; all three capital observatories set records. Sources: [dsalicante.com](https://dsalicante.com/noticias/alicante/verano-mas-caluroso-alicante/) (headline: "hubo 15 noches por encima de 25 grados"); [tiempo.com/Meteored, summarising AEMET](https://www.tiempo.com/ram/verano-climatico-2026-comunidad-valenciana.html). One search summary wrongly called these "tropical nights"; the headline confirms the >25 °C threshold.
- **Regional (Comunitat Valenciana) figures from AEMET for summer 2026:**
  - Mean 26.0 °C, 2.7 °C above the 23.3 °C normal.
  - Warmest since at least 1950 and driest since 2016.
  - 45 of 92 days set a daily record in the series since at least 1940.
  - Five *aviso rojo* (red warning) days for maximum temperatures: 7, 8, 15 and 23 July and 19 August. This was the first time since Meteoalerta began that five red-warning days occurred in one year.
  - Sources: [tiempo.com](https://www.tiempo.com/ram/verano-climatico-2026-comunidad-valenciana.html); [El Periòdic](https://www.elperiodic.com/confirmado-comunitat-valenciana-vivido-verano-calido-jamas-registrado-estos-datos-corroboran_1093680).
  - **CONFLICT:** another search summary attributed "26.0 °C, +2.7 °C" to the Alicante observatory itself. tiempo.com and dsalicante present it as the regional average, and a 23.3 °C normal fits a region that includes inland areas, not the coastal city. The Alicante-city summer 2026 mean was not retrieved.
- In the region, summer 2026 brought "several extreme episodes that pushed thermometers above 44 and 45 °C". This applies to the Comunitat as a whole, not necessarily Alicante city. The ten hottest summers of the last 77 years all fall in this century, and summer 2026 was also the hottest across Spain. Sources: [TodoAlicante, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.todoalicante.es/el-tiempo/alicante-registra-verano-caluroso-historia-supera-2025-20260921140411-nt.html); [El Periòdic](https://www.elperiodic.com/confirmado-comunitat-valenciana-vivido-verano-calido-jamas-registrado-estos-datos-corroboran_1093680) *(attribution uncertain)*.
- Early season: by 24 June 2026 Alicante city had already logged 15 tropical nights (minimum ≥20 °C). AEMET's seasonal outlook had forecast a very hot summer with more tropical nights and storm rainfall. Source: [Diario de Alicante](https://diariodealicante.net/verano-muy-calido-comunitat-valenciana-noches-tropicales-tormentas/) *(attribution uncertain)*. By late June the sea was the warmest since 1940: [TodoAlicante, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.todoalicante.es/alicante-provincia/mar-calido-1940-anticipa-verano-sofocante-alicante-20260624082033-nt.html); [English version, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.todoalicante.es/english/warmest-since-1940-predicts-20260626060658-nt.html).

**2025**
- By 11 July 2025 Alicante city had recorded 36 tropical nights in the year, described as a record pace ("Alicante bate el récord anual de noches con temperatura tropical en solo 20 días de verano"). Source: [TodoAlicante, 11 Jul 2025](https://www.todoalicante.es/el-tiempo/tiempo-alicante-noches-calidas-temperaturas-20250711135108-nt.html).
- By 22 October 2025 there had been **105 tropical nights** in 2025. According to AEMET, this was the first year ever above 100. The summary says "province"; the observatory series is most likely meant *(attribution uncertain)*. Source: [TodoAlicante, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.todoalicante.es/el-tiempo/aemet-activa-alerta-amarilla-viento-alicante-20251022115355-nt.html).
- Summer 2025 was then the hottest on record for Spain as a whole, per AEMET: [AOL/Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/spains-2025-summer-hottest-record-130501289.html). It is now the second hottest in Alicante after 2026: [TodoAlicante, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.todoalicante.es/el-tiempo/alicante-registra-verano-caluroso-historia-supera-2025-20260921140411-nt.html).
- A long-term trend article covers 45 years of rising summer temperatures in Alicante, based on AEMET data; its figures were not retrieved. Source: [El Español, 17 Aug 2025](https://www.elespanol.com/alicante/20250817/anos-aumento-temperaturas-estivales-alicante-registrado-aemet/1003743886342_0.html).

**Records and heat events**
- 3 Oct 2024: 34.2 °C at Alicante-Elche airport, the second-highest October value in its AEMET series after 34.3 °C on 5 Oct 2015. It was reported as the second-hottest October day since 1946 ("cifras no vistas en casi 80 años"). Source: [COPE Alicante, 3 Oct 2024](https://www.cope.es/emisoras/comunidad-valenciana/alicante-provincia/alicante/cope-alicante/noticias/alicante-registra-segundo-dia-caluroso-octubre-1946-20241003_3021507.html).
- July 2025 *poniente* (dry westerly) episode:
  - AEMET forecast temperatures "not seen in three years".
  - At 11:00 the Alicante observatory already exceeded 35 °C, with relative humidity of 17% and westerly gusts of 25 km/h.
  - Sea breezes arrived only after midday.
  - Source: [TodoAlicante, 19 Jul 2025](https://www.todoalicante.es/el-tiempo/tiempo-alicante-poniente-temperatura-calor-20250719122432-nt.html) *(attribution of the 11:00 observation uncertain)*.
- The provincial absolute maximum is cited as 44.9 °C at Orihuela on 25 July 2022. Sources: [x-y.es](https://x-y.es/clima/alacant/) / [meteosolana](https://es.meteosolana.net/estacion/8019). These are secondary sources, the attribution is uncertain, and the figure was not checked against AEMET.

### Inferences
- Two consecutive record summers and more than 100 tropical nights in 2025 mean the 1991–2020 normals understate current summer heat, especially at night. For planning, treat 2025–2026 as the new typical summer rather than relying on the normals.
- In a hot year the family should expect roughly two weeks of nights at 25 °C or above. With 2025 as a guide, tropical nights can run from about June to October. Bedroom air conditioning for a 4–5-year-old is effectively mandatory, not optional.

### Gaps
- The AEMET 1991–2020 monthly tables for 8025 and 8019 (mean max/min, relative humidity, precipitation, sunshine hours, clear days, days ≥30 °C) are blocked and must be read directly. Annual precipitation for Alicante was **not retrieved at all**.
- Counts of days ≥30 °C and of tropical and torrid nights for 2022, 2023 and 2024 were not retrieved. A background AEMET document on tropical nights exists and was not read: [AEMET CV blog PDF, 2020](https://aemetblog.es/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/noches_tropicales-1.pdf).
- The official Alicante-city mean for summers 2025 and 2026 was not retrieved (only the regional 26.0 °C). AEMET's seasonal-analysis page for Alicante exists but was not readable: [AEMET análisis estacional 8025](https://www.aemet.es/en/serviciosclimaticos/vigilancia_clima/analisis_estacional?l=8025).
- Absolute temperature records for stations 8025 and 8019 were not retrieved.

## Q2. Summer comfort outdoors: heat plus humidity, sea breeze, poniente, sea temperature, daily routine with a young child

### Takeaway
Alicante's summer heat is humid rather than extreme on the thermometer. The sea breeze caps daytime maxima but raises humidity, so the heat index runs 4–6 °C above the air temperature, and nights stay at or above 20–25 °C. Dry *poniente* days bring the hottest, driest mornings. The sea averages about 27 °C in August and reached about 29–30 °C in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- AEMET Comunitat Valenciana example: in Alicante, 31.7 °C with 62% relative humidity gave a heat index of **37.0 °C**. AEMET's comment: "the breeze cools temperatures but also raises humidity, so the sensation of heat increases". Source: [AEMET CV on X, Aug 2019](https://x.com/aemet_cvalencia/status/1159486867492036608).
- July 2026: Alicante's average relative humidity never fell below 70%, with days at 80–85% ("typical of equatorial climates"). A specialist quoted in the piece: "with 27 °C and 80% humidity the body feels 31–32 °C". Alicante now feels hotter than the Guadalquivir Valley because its nights do not cool, unlike Seville's. Source: [El Español, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/alicante/alicante-ciudad/20260814/noches-ecuatoriales-humedad-extrema-alicante-calurosa-valle-guadalquivir/1003744352515_0.html).
- In summer 2026 a record-warm sea (+2.5 °C anomaly in the Balearic Sea zone) "inhibited the breezes" and helped drive the torrid-night record. Sources: [dsalicante.com](https://dsalicante.com/noticias/alicante/verano-mas-caluroso-alicante/); [tiempo.com](https://www.tiempo.com/ram/verano-climatico-2026-comunidad-valenciana.html). The +2.5 °C anomaly made it the warmest summer sea since at least 1940 (tiempo.com). Across the zone, mean sea temperature exceeded 29 °C from 6 to 20 August, peaking at 29.8 °C on 17 August. Source: [dsalicante.com](https://dsalicante.com/noticias/alicante/verano-mas-caluroso-alicante/) *(attribution uncertain; figures are zone-wide, not specific to Alicante)*.
- The poniente (dry westerly) is the main driver of stifling heat days in Alicante. In July 2025 AEMET warned of persistent poniente with breezes arriving only after midday. Source: [TodoAlicante, 19 Jul 2025](https://www.todoalicante.es/el-tiempo/tiempo-alicante-poniente-temperatura-calor-20250719122432-nt.html).
- Sea-surface temperature at Alicante:
  - August average ≈27 °C, ranging from 25.3 to 29.6 °C. Source: [seatemperature.info](https://seatemperature.info/es/agosto/alicante-temperatura-del-agua-del-mar.html) (secondary, modelled).
  - Winter ≈14–16 °C, with February among the coldest months. Source: [temperaturadelmar.es](https://www.temperaturadelmar.es/europa/espana/alicante/when.html) (secondary; *attribution uncertain*).

### Inferences
- A practical routine for July–August (inference from the heat-index and poniente findings, not an official recommendation):
  - Outdoor play and beach from about 07:00 to 10:30, then again after about 19:00 in parks and on the promenade.
  - From about 12:00 to 17:30, stay indoors or in air-conditioned places (museums, shopping centres) or in the water in shade.
  - On poniente days, cut even the morning slot short, since it was already above 35 °C at 11:00 in July 2025.
  - On breeze days the midday feels muggy rather than scorching, with the heat index about 5 °C above the air temperature.
- June and September are the comfortable "summer" months. Given current sea temperatures, the bathing season plausibly runs June to October.
- Compared with Bali: in recent summers, July–August nights in Alicante approach Bali-like mugginess (relative humidity 70–85%, nights 20–25 °C+). The difference is that this lasts about three months rather than all year, and winters are cool and drier.

### Gaps
- No AEMET hourly climatology (temperature and humidity by hour), no count of poniente days per year, and no official monthly sea-temperature table. The Puertos del Estado buoy for Alicante was not retrieved.
- UV-index statistics were not retrieved.

## Q4. Extreme weather: DANA/torrential rain and floods, drought and water supply, calima

### Takeaway
Alicante city was largely spared by the catastrophic DANA of 29 Oct 2024, which devastated Valencia province. DANA "Alice" on 9–10 Oct 2025, however, brought red warnings and more than 200 l/m² locally in the south of the province (Vega Baja), with flooding in Alicante and Murcia. Drinking-water supply is protected by desalination (MCT) and has run without restrictions. Saharan dust (calima) episodes occur several times a year, mostly in winter and spring.

### Cited Findings
- **DANA, 29 Oct 2024:**
  - Alicante province was only under yellow warnings, while Valencia had orange and red.
  - In Alicante city, the Castillo de Santa Bárbara, El Palmeral and Lo Morant parks were closed. Source: [El Español, 29 Oct 2024](https://www.elespanol.com/alicante/20241029/dana-azota-alicante-fuertes-lluvias-vientos-activa-avisos-amarillos-aemet/897160316_0.html).
  - The DANA's core sat between Andalusia and Extremadura, making Valencia the most unstable area; Alicante was affected mainly by wind. Source: [TodoAlicante, 30 Oct 2024](https://www.todoalicante.es/el-tiempo/dana-pasado-largo-alicante-20241030214701-nt.html).
  - The north of the province had tail-end storms that weekend. Source: [TodoAlicante, 30 Oct 2024](https://www.todoalicante.es/alicante-provincia/dana-dara-ultimos-coletazos-fin-semana-tormentas-20241030142750-nt.html).
  - Note: the "≈400 l/m²" figure and the flooding of the A-7 at km 376 (Montortal) that appeared in the same search summary refer to **Valencia** province, not Alicante. Overview: [Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inundaciones_de_la_DANA_de_2024_en_Espa%C3%B1a).
- **DANA "Alice", 9–10 Oct 2025:**
  - AEMET issued a red warning for Friday 10 Oct 2025, 10:00–24:00, on the southern coast of Alicante, for up to 60 l/m² in one hour.
  - More than 180 l/m² in 12 hours was expected across Valencia, Castellón, Alicante and Murcia; some areas exceeded 200 l/m².
  - The Vega Baja del Segura was worst hit, with streets, homes and shops flooded in Orihuela, Torrevieja, Pilar de la Horadada and Dolores. Barrancos and streets flooded in Alicante and Murcia, flights were diverted, and there were closures and civil-protection alerts.
  - Sources: [Telecinco, 9 Oct 2025](https://www.telecinco.es/noticias/tiempo/20251009/dana-alice-ultima-hora-temporal-inundaciones-directo_18_016821948.html); [Estrella Digital, 10 Oct 2025](https://www.estrelladigital.es/articulo/sociedad/aviso-rojo-lluvias-torrenciales-alicante-murcia-llegada-dana-alice/20251010080353436529.html); [eltiempo.es](https://www.eltiempo.es/noticias/dana-alice-en-alicante-aviso-rojo-cierres-vuelos-desviados-y-alertas-de-proteccion-civil).
- 16 Sep 2026: AEMET orange warning for storms and heavy rain in Alicante. Source: [El Español, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/alicante/20260916/aemet-activa-aviso-naranja-tormentas-fuertes-lluvias-jueves-alicante/1003744385796_0.html). AEMET expects an "abnormal" autumn 2026 after the record summer: [El Periòdic](https://www.elperiodic.com/verano-calido-historia-otono-fuera-normal-esto-espera-aemet-comunitat-valenciana_1093727) (details not retrieved).
- Alicante meteorologists warn that climate change will make DANAs and droughts ever more extreme. Source: [El Español, 31 Oct 2024](https://www.elespanol.com/alicante/20241031/alerta-meteorologos-alicante-cambio-climatico-provocara-dana-sequias-vez-extremas/897410533_0.html).
- **Water supply:**
  - The Mancomunidad de los Canales del Taibilla (MCT) supplies 80 municipalities and more than 2.5 million permanent residents, almost half of them in Alicante province.
  - Despite drought in the basin, its mix of resources, including desalination, has guaranteed supply without restrictions. The Secretary of State for the Environment said no restrictions are expected in the short, medium or long term thanks to desalination.
  - Source: [iAgua, MCT profile](https://www.iagua.es/mancomunidad-de-los-canales-del-taibilla) (statement date not shown).
  - The MCT runs four desalination plants: Alicante I, Alicante II (designed for 65,000 m³/day) and two at San Pedro del Pinatar. An expansion of Alicante I is planned. Sources: [MCT Alicante II](https://www.mct.es/web/mct/alicante-ii); [MCT desaladoras](https://www.mct.es/web/mct/desaladoras); [MCT Alicante I ampliación](https://www.mct.es/web/mct/desalinizadora-alicante-i-ampliacion).
- **Calima:** 5–10 episodes a year, peaking in winter and spring, each typically lasting 1–3 days. Source: [calimacanarias.com](https://calimacanarias.com/peninsula/alicante/) (secondary, low authority). A November 2025 episode was attributed to Saharan air pushed from North Africa, according to Olcina, a meteorologist ([El Español, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.elespanol.com/alicante/vivir/20251114/olcina-meteorologo-calima-alicante-debe-entrada-aire-sahariano-empujado-norte-africa/1003744013731_0.html)). Calima returned to Spain in late May 2026 ([Libertad Digital, 29 May 2026](https://www.libertaddigital.com/ciencia-tecnologia/salud/2026-05-29/vuelve-la-calima-a-espana-prevision-del-polvo-sahariano-7411196/)). Calima raises PM10 and PM2.5.

### Inferences
- Within the province, flood risk is concentrated in the Vega Baja (Orihuela–Torrevieja), south of Alicante city. In the city itself the realistic risk is flash flooding of streets and ramblas during intense autumn storms (September–November). A family should avoid ground-floor or basement flats near ramblas and low-lying streets and should check official flood maps (SNCZI / PATRICOVA; not researched here).
- Water scarcity is a regional agricultural and political issue but, according to the MCT and the government, not a household supply risk, thanks to desalination.

### Gaps
- Rainfall totals and damage in Alicante city itself during DANA Alice were not retrieved (only province-level reports).
- Historical city flood events and SNCZI/PATRICOVA risk zones for specific neighbourhoods were not checked.
- The dated drought status in 2025–2026 (the Segura/Júcar plan de sequía) was not retrieved.
- AEMET or CAMS statistics on calima days per year were not retrieved; the 5–10 figure comes from a low-authority site.

## Q5. Air quality: Generalitat monitoring (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, O3) vs WHO 2021; EEA city ranking

### Takeaway
Official Generalitat annual averages for 2024 and 2025 **were not retrieved**. The city reports good air in the industrial area of Las Atalayas, "good" on 98% of days. IQAir (unofficial) lists an annual PM2.5 of 4.5 µg/m³, which would be within the WHO guideline of 5. The EEA changed its city-ranking method in 2025, and the Alicante/Elche decile was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Air quality at Las Atalayas, an industrial estate, was rated "good" on 98% of days over the previous two years. Source: [Ayuntamiento de Alicante](https://www.alicante.es/es/noticias/calidad-del-aire-atalayas-fue-buena-98-dias-ultimos-dos-anos) (date not shown).
- The city has three automatic Generalitat monitoring stations. In addition, six Port Authority stations around the Port of Alicante, measuring PM10 and PM2.5, have been incorporated into the Generalitat network. Source: [Ayuntamiento de Alicante, Calidad del Aire](https://www.alicante.es/es/contenidos/calidad-del-aire) *(attribution uncertain)*.
- The University of Alicante's 2024 campus air-quality report draws on Generalitat stations in Agost and in Alicante (El Pla, La Florida, Rabasa). Source: [UA, Informe calidad del aire 2024 (PDF)](https://web.ua.es/es/ecocampus/documentos/calidad-ambiental-ua/calidad-aire-ua/informe-calidad-aire-ua-2024.pdf) (values not retrieved).
- IQAir: annual PM2.5 of 4.5 µg/m³ for Alicante, within the WHO 2021 guideline of 5 µg/m³. Source: [IQAir](https://www.iqair.com/es/spain/valencia/alacant). This is **not an official regulatory figure**: IQAir mixes sensors and models, and the year is likely 2024 but not verified. Treat with caution.
- The official national assessment "Evaluación de la calidad del aire en España, Año 2024" (MITECO, July 2025) exists; Alicante-zone values were not retrieved. ## Q9. Would the family feel isolated? Accessibility vs Valencia, Málaga, Lisbon

### Takeaway
Within Europe, no. ALC is a roughly 20-million-passenger airport with about 140–150 destinations in about 32 countries, and Madrid is 2 h 20 min away by train. For the family's long-distance ties (Russia, Bali), Alicante is **not** a direct gateway. Russia means one or more connections, including the four-a-week Pegasus Istanbul flight. Bali means at least two connections, or positioning to Madrid first. The comparison with Málaga, Valencia and Lisbon could not be quantified in this session.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Everyday European mobility (weekend trips, visiting clients in London, Amsterdam, Berlin and similar) is excellent and cheap, though dominated by low-cost carriers. Winter capacity is also high (4.2 million seats), so the city does not "shut down" in the off-season.
- Long-haul isolation is the real weakness. Every trip to Russia or Asia starts with a connection at Madrid, Barcelona, Istanbul or elsewhere. A family used to Bali's direct Gulf and Asian links should expect longer, more complex journeys home, and Russian passport holders face routing constraints; visas are outside this note's scope.
