## 1. Legal basis and the 2026 rulebook (Ley 14/2013 + Ley 28/2022; RD 1155/2024; 2025–2026 changes)

### Takeaway
The DNV is the *visado / autorización de residencia para teletrabajo de carácter internacional*. It sits in Ley 14/2013 (Título V, Sección 2ª, new Capítulo V bis, art. 74 bis and following), which was inserted by Ley 28/2022 ("Ley de Startups"). The new Reglamento de Extranjería (RD 1155/2024, in force 20 May 2025) does **not** regulate this permit, so the DNV rules themselves did not change in 2025. The practical 2026 changes for this family are:
- the higher income threshold that follows from **SMI 2026 = €1,221 × 14** (RD 126/2026);
- the EU's **Nov 2025 tightening of multiple-entry Schengen visas** for Russians residing in Russia (§7).

### Cited Findings
- Ley 28/2022 added a new Chapter V bis, "International Teleworkers", to Section 2 of Title V of Ley 14/2013. — [Iberley, "Nuevo visado y autorización de residencia para el teletrabajo de carácter internacional"](https://www.iberley.es/noticias/nuevo-visado-autorizacion-residencia-teletrabajo-caracter-internacional-32071) [SECONDARY-legal database]; law text: [BOE-A-2022-21739, Ley 28/2022, de 21 de diciembre](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739) [OFFICIAL]

- RD 1155/2024 (new Reglamento of LO 4/2000) was published in the BOE on 20 Nov 2024 and has been in force since **20 May 2025**. ## 2. Income requirement 2026 (SMI 2026 → family threshold) vs $5,000–6,000 (and $8,000) per month

### Takeaway
- **SMI 2026 = €1,221/month in 14 payments = €17,094/year** (RD 126/2026, BOE 19 Feb 2026, effective from 1 Jan 2026).
- On the UGE basis the family-of-3 threshold is **300% of annual SMI = €51,282/year = €4,273.50/month**.
- **$5,000/month is borderline:** it equals the threshold at about 1.17 USD/EUR and falls short if the euro is stronger.
- **$6,000 gives roughly a 12–28% cushion; $8,000 is comfortable** (roughly 50–70% above).

### Cited Findings
- **Real Decreto 126/2026, de 18 de febrero**, fixes the SMI for 2026 (BOE-A-2026-3815, BOE of 19 Feb 2026). The figures are:
  - **€1,221/month in 14 payments**, a rise of **3.1%**;
  - **€17,094/year**;
  - **€40.70/day**;
  - **€1,424.50/month** if extra payments are prorated over 12 months.

  It entered into force on **20 Feb 2026** with effect from **1 Jan to 31 Dec 2026**. — [BOE-A-2026-3815](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2026-3815) [OFFICIAL]; [SEPE, Feb 2026](https://sepe.es/noticia/SEPE/2026/Febrero/boe-publica-smi-2026) [OFFICIAL]; [Iberley](https://www.iberley.es/noticias/publicado-salario-minimo-interprofesional-2026-36093); [Sagardoy](https://sagardoy.com/salario-minimo-interprofesional-para-2026/); [La Moncloa, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/trabajo14/paginas/2023/140223-salario-minimo-interprofesional.aspx)
- DNV thresholds for 2026 as reported by law firms:
  - 200% of SMI = **€34,188/year ≈ €2,849/month**;
  - **+75% of SMI** for the first family member (≈ **€1,068/month**);
  - **+25% of SMI** for each additional member (≈ **€356/month**).

  — [Cohen y Aguirre](https://cohenyaguirre.es/nuevos-requisitos-visado-de-nomada-digital); [Expedientes Extranjería](https://expedientesextranjeria.com/visado-nomada-digital-espana-2026/); [MSR Abogados](https://msr-abogados.com/digital-nomad-visa-spain/); [Jobbatical, "Spain DNV 2026: €2,849 Income Guide"](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/spain-digital-nomad-visa-income-smi-increase) [SECONDARY]
- Contradiction: some consulates (reported for US consulates) ask for **€2,442/month**, which is 2 × €1,221 on the 14-payment monthly basis, instead of the UGE figure of €2,849 (= 2 × €17,094 / 12). — [España Abogados, nómada digital](https://espanaabogados.com/servicios/nomada-digital/); [Jobbatical](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/spain-digital-nomad-visa-income-smi-increase) [SECONDARY]
- Income must be **recurring**. A one-off bank balance is not enough; applicants should show invoices or payslips from the **last three months**, plus contracts or company certificates. — law-firm guides as above ([Expedientes Extranjería](https://expedientesextranjeria.com/visado-nomada-digital-espana-2026/), [Integrate Abroad](https://integrateabroad.com/blog/visa-nomada-digital-espana-requisitos)) [SECONDARY]
- Data-quality flag: one aggregated search summary quoted "1,121 €" for SMI 2026. That is a typo; the BOE and SEPE figure is €1,221.

## 3. Other requirements (qualification, client relationship, company age, 20% rule, remote-work proof, social security incl. the Spain–Russia convention, health insurance, criminal records, no irregular stay)

### Takeaway
The father must show:
- a degree, **or ≥3 years of experience in analogous functions**;
- commercial contracts of **≥3 months** with foreign companies that **authorize remote work from Spain**;
- **≤20%** of his professional activity for Spanish companies;
- private insurance **with no copays and no waiting periods**, or Spanish Social Security cover;
- clean **criminal records for the last 2 years**, plus a sworn statement covering 5 years;
- as a self-employed person, **registration in RETA**.

The 1994 Spain–Russia social security convention is in force but is **not a practical route** for a Russian freelancer living in Bali. Plan on RETA.

### Cited Findings
- **Qualification:** a university degree or postgraduate title from universities, vocational training or business schools of recognized prestige (foreign titles apostilled), **or at least 3 years of professional experience in functions analogous to the remote job**. — [Santiago de Chile consulate requirements PDF](https://www.exteriores.gob.es/Consulados/santiagodechile/es/ServiciosConsulares/Documents/Requisitos%20visado%20TEL.pdf) [OFFICIAL]; [Machelín Díaz on art. 74 bis](https://machelindiaz.com/teletrabajadores-internacionales-en-espana-actualizaciones-del-articulo-74-bis-de-la-ley-14-2013/); [Iberley, "Teletrabajadores de carácter internacional"](https://www.iberley.es/temas/teletrabajadores-caracter-internacional-67187) [SECONDARY]
- **20% rule:** in the case of a professional (self-employed) activity, the holder may work for a company located in Spain provided that work does not exceed **20% of total professional activity**. — same sources as above
- **Self-employed (autónomo) eligibility:** a self-employed person counts as an international teleworker if they prove a professional relationship through a **commercial contract with the foreign company for at least three months** and **the company authorizes remote work from Spain**. They must **register in RETA**. The initial application includes the signed **"MIT" form**. — [UGE, "Teletrabajadores"](https://www.inclusion.gob.es/en/web/unidadgrandesempresas/teletrabajadores) and [UGE document "información y documentación – titulares"](https://www.inclusion.gob.es/documents/d/unidadgrandesempresas/informacion-documentacion-titulares) [OFFICIAL, via search snippet]
- **Remote-work proof:** UGE-CE can issue *requerimientos* (requests for more documents) about the position, company or activity to verify that the work can really be done remotely. — [España Abogados, "Requerimientos UGE-CE teletrabajador internacional 2026: los 7 casos"](https://espanaabogados.com/requerimientos-uge-ce-teletrabajador-internacional/) [SECONDARY]
- **Criminal records (holder):** certificate(s) covering the **last 2 years**, plus a **declaración responsable** (sworn statement) of no criminal record for the **last 5 years**. **Family members:** the same, "salvo excepciones" (save exceptions). — [infoextranjeria, 2 Jul 2025, "Documentación teletrabajadores… titulares y familiares"](https://infoextranjeria.org/2025/07/02/documentacion-teletrabajadores-de-caracter-internacional-titulares-y-familiares/) [SECONDARY, summarizing UGE documentation]
- **Health insurance:** public cover, or private health insurance with an insurer authorized to operate in Spain. **Not accepted:** travel insurance, reimbursement-only policies, and policies with **waiting periods (carencias) or copays (copagos)**. — [infoextranjeria, 2 Jul 2025](https://infoextranjeria.org/2025/07/02/documentacion-teletrabajadores-de-caracter-internacional-titulares-y-familiares/) [SECONDARY]
- **Social security:**
  - For freelancers, **RETA registration is mandatory once the authorization is granted and before starting activity**.
  - The only alternative is a certificate of applicable legislation (certificado de cobertura) under a bilateral agreement that **expressly covers remote work from Spain**.
  - **UGE-CE does not accept a mere application** for such a certificate.
  - Only some agreement countries issue these certificates for teleworkers.
  - The April 2023 Instrucción conjunta sets the rules for Spanish SS registration or exemption and requires health cover for the whole stay.

  — [Machelín Díaz, "Nómada digital y Seguridad Social internacional"](https://machelindiaz.com/nomada-digital-y-seguridad-social-internacional-a1-acuerdo-marco-ue-2023-y-que-exige-uge/); [Entre Trámites, "Certificado de cobertura para DNV"](https://entretramites.com/extranjeria/nomada-certificado-de-cobertura); [AS Abogada](https://asabogada.com/nomada-digital/) [SECONDARY]
- **Spain–Russia Social Security Convention:**
  - signed in Madrid on **11 Apr 1994**;
  - Administrative Agreement signed in Moscow on **12 May 1995**;
  - **in force since 22 Feb 1996**;
  - posting certificates exist in bilingual form **E/R.1**.

  — [BOE-A-1996-4208](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-1996-4208) [OFFICIAL]; [Xunta de Galicia, Secretaría Xeral da Emigración](https://emigracion.xunta.gal/es/la-secretaria/normativa/convenio-bilateral-entre-espana-y-rusia-materia-seguridad-social) [OFFICIAL-regional]; [Form E/R.1 (MAZ)](https://www.maz.es/Formularios%20e%20impresos/Formularios%20e%20impresos/ER1.pdf)

## 4. Proof of income for a freelancer (documents, months, payment channels, Russian banks)

### Takeaway
UGE wants **recurring** income. In practice that means:
- commercial contracts with each foreign client;
- client letters confirming the relationship and authorizing remote work from Spain;
- invoices for **at least the last 3 months** (lawyers typically advise 6–12);
- bank or e-money statements matching the invoices.

Payments that pass through Payoneer or Wise should be documented with official statements in the holder's name. Crypto income and income into Russian banks are weak evidence and a sanctions and compliance risk.

### Cited Findings
- Income can be proved with contracts, payslips or company certificates. It must be **recurring** rather than a one-time balance, with invoices or payslips from the **last 3 months**. — [Expedientes Extranjería](https://expedientesextranjeria.com/visado-nomada-digital-espana-2026/); [Integrate Abroad](https://integrateabroad.com/blog/visa-nomada-digital-espana-requisitos); [Cohen y Aguirre](https://cohenyaguirre.es/nuevos-requisitos-visado-de-nomada-digital) [SECONDARY]
- The self-employed route needs **commercial contract(s) of ≥3 months** with the foreign company(ies), plus the company's authorization of remote work from Spain. — [UGE teletrabajadores](https://www.inclusion.gob.es/en/web/unidadgrandesempresas/teletrabajadores) [OFFICIAL via snippet]
- A 2026 law-firm article lists the "7 typical cases" of UGE requerimientos for teleworkers. The title was retrieved but the content could not be. — [España Abogados, 2026](https://espanaabogados.com/requerimientos-uge-ce-teletrabajador-internacional/) [SECONDARY]
- Common causes of refusal for teleworkers are discussed by a Madrid immigration firm (title retrieved only). — [Machelín Díaz, "errores que provocan denegaciones"](https://machelindiaz.com/teletrabajadores-internacionales-en-espana-requisitos-documentos-y-errores-que-provocan-denegaciones/) [SECONDARY]

### Inferences
- **Recommended proof pack**, derived from the cited rules:
  1. A contract per main client: start date, open-ended or ≥12-month term, scope (motion design), fee, and a remote-work clause. At least one client relationship should be **≥3 months old** at filing.
  2. A letter from each main client on letterhead confirming the relationship, fees and remote work from Spain.
  3. Each client's company registry extract showing ≥1 year of existence (see §3 Gaps).
  4. Invoices for 3 months minimum; **6–12 months is safer**.
  5. Bank or e-money statements showing the matching receipts.
  6. Any tax filings for the income, e.g. Russian НПД/ИП declarations (not required by law but persuasive).
  7. Russian-language documents apostilled and translated.
- **Keep Spanish clients ≤20%** of activity. Since his clients are outside Spain this is not currently an issue.
- **Clients should be companies.** The law speaks of "empresas", so income from private individuals or marketplace micro-gigs is weaker evidence.
- **At $5,000/month** the evidence should show stable months with no dips (see §2).

### Gaps
- Acceptance of Wise, Payoneer or crypto receipts by UGE in 2025–2026 was not verified; no source was retrieved.
  - BG-Med: e-money statements (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) are generally accepted as account statements if they show the holder's name and the transactions.
  - Crypto-only income is widely considered hard to prove and risky under AML rules. No official rule was found.
- **Russian banks:** BG-High.
  - Since 2022 several major Russian banks have been disconnected from SWIFT.
  - Russian-issued Visa and Mastercard cards do not work abroad.
  - Wise stopped serving Russia-resident customers in 2022.
  - Foreign clients paying into Russian banks is therefore impractical and would look risky in a Spanish file.

## 9. Path to long-term residence (residencia de larga duración / larga duración-UE) after 5 years

### Takeaway
DNV residence counts toward the **5 years of continuous legal residence** required for long-term residence. The key risk is **absences**: BG-High, no more than 6 months at a time and no more than 10 months in total over the 5 years. The EU long-term status (larga duración-UE) also requires stable resources and health insurance.

## 10. Path to Spanish citizenship: 10 years for Russians, DELE A2 + CCSE, timelines, renunciation of Russian citizenship, options for the child, 2025–2026 reforms

### Takeaway
Russians need **10 years of legal, continuous residence immediately before applying**. They must pass **DELE A2 (or higher) + CCSE**, show good civic conduct, and at the oath formally **declare renunciation** of Russian nationality. Spain does not require actual loss of Russian nationality, and Russia does not recognize that declaration.

For this family, the earliest application is about **2036–2037** (10 years after the first permit), with a decision 1–3 years later. The child can then **opt** for Spanish nationality through the parents (art. 20 CC) or apply on their own residence.

### Cited Findings
- No 2025–2026 sources were retrieved; the search budget was exhausted before this section. Everything below is background knowledge that needs verification.

### Inferences
- **Clock:** if the first permit (UGE 3-year) is granted around early 2027, 10 years is reached around early 2037. If processing takes 1–3 years, nationality comes around **2038–2040**, when the parents are about 36–38 and the child about 16–19.
- Absences and gaps in legal residence (late renewals, irregular periods) reset or weaken the "continuous" requirement.

### Gaps
All items below are unverified background.
- **Residence periods (Código Civil art. 22):** BG-High. **10 years** is the general rule. Shorter periods (2 years) apply to Ibero-Americans, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea, Portugal and Sephardic Jews; 5 years to refugees; 1 year to those born in Spain, married to a Spaniard, etc. Russians therefore need 10. The residence must be legal, continuous and immediately prior to the application. Verify in [Código Civil, BOE-A-1889-4763](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1889-4763).
- **Integration exams:** BG-High. **DELE A2+ and CCSE** (Instituto Cervantes) prove integration (Ley 19/2015, RD 1004/2015). Minors under 18 are exempt.
- **Fee and processing time:** BG-Med. The nationality fee (modelo 790 código 026) is about **€104.05**. Applications are electronic to the Ministerio de Justicia. The legal deadline is 1 year with negative silence; in practice 1–3 years.
- **Renunciation and oath (art. 23 CC):** BG-High. The applicant swears loyalty to the King and obedience to the Constitution, **declares renunciation of the prior nationality** (Russia is not exempt), and registers in the Civil Registry. Spain does not demand proof of loss of Russian nationality.
- **Loss of Spanish nationality (art. 25.1.a CC):** BG-High. A naturalized Spaniard **loses Spanish nationality if for 3 years they use exclusively the nationality they declared to renounce**. Using the Russian passport exclusively (e.g. for travel) is therefore risky.
- **Russian side:** BG-Med.
  - Under the Federal Law on Citizenship **138-FZ** (28 Apr 2023, in force 26 Oct 2023), the person remains Russian unless formally renouncing through a Russian consulate (possible conditions: no unfulfilled obligations).
  - Russian citizens must **notify the MVD of a second citizenship or a residence document within 60 days**; the deadline is linked to being in Russia. Non-notification carries criminal liability under **art. 330.2 of the Russian Criminal Code**.
  - Whether a Spanish *temporary* TIE counts as a notifiable "residence document" is debated. Long-term residence and Spanish nationality clearly do.
- **Child (art. 20 CC):** BG-High.
  - A person who is or has been under the parental authority (patria potestad) of a Spaniard may **opt** for Spanish nationality. Under 14 the legal representative declares, with Civil Registry authorization; from 14 to 18 the minor declares, assisted.
  - The right **expires at age 20**.
  - Alternatively, the child can naturalize on 10 years of their own residence.
  - A **second child born in Spain** would need only **1 year** of residence (art. 22.2.a).
- **2025–2026 reforms** to nationality law (e.g. proposals to change the 10-year rule or the exams) were not verified.

## 11. Russia-specific complications (documents and apostille from abroad, EU sanctions and banking, payments, scrutiny, Russian consular services in Spain, policy debates, military-age issues)

### Takeaway
The main friction points:
1. obtaining and **apostilling Russian documents while living in Bali**, since apostilles are issued only inside Russia;

  - The Russian criminal-record certificate ("справка о наличии/отсутствии судимости") can be ordered on **Gosuslugi**. The electronic version carries the MVD e-signature, but a paper original is normally needed for an **apostille by the MVD** (ИЦ/ГИАЦ).
  - From abroad, it can be requested through a Russian embassy or consulate; this takes weeks to months.
  - The **Indonesian SKCK** is issued by Polri (Baintelkam). Indonesia joined the Apostille Convention with effect from 4 Jun 2022 (BG-High), so the SKCK can be apostilled in Indonesia.
  - Sworn translation (Russian/Indonesian → Spanish) is done by a traductor jurado from the MAEC list.
- **Scrutiny or refusal rates for Russians at UGE and consulates (2025–2026):** no evidence found (search budget exhausted). Unknown, and not to be asserted.
- **Russian consular services in Spain:** BG-Med. The Embassy consular section is in Madrid and there is a Consulate General in Barcelona. Appointments are booked via kdmid.ru. Passport renewal abroad is possible (10-year biometric passports), but queues and waiting times in 2026 were not verified.
- **Military-age exposure:** BG-Med.
  - Since the April 2023 amendments (electronic summonses and a unified military register), a man who receives a summons can be **barred from leaving Russia**.
  - The conscription age has been **18–30 since 1 Jan 2024** (BG-High).
  - A 24-year-old father travelling to Russia (for Moscow or St Petersburg consular filing, or for documents) carries this risk. Consider routes that avoid travel to Russia.
- **EU and Spanish policy debates on restricting residence permits for Russians:** not found. BG-Low: EU debates in 2025–2026 focused on visas and on entry bans for Russian ex-combatants, not on residence permits. Unverified.

## 12. Alternatives if the DNV does not fit (brief comparison)

### Takeaway
For this family the DNV is the best fit:
- it allows remote work for foreign clients;
- it gives a 3-year permit when filed in Spain;
- the family's work rights are widely reported (verify).

The alternatives are all worse for a remote freelancer:
- **no lucrativa** forbids work;
- **cuenta propia** targets a Spain-based business and needs a viability report;
- **highly qualified professional** needs a Spanish employer;
- **ENISA entrepreneur** needs an innovative business plan;
- **student routes** tie him to studies and count less toward long-term residence and citizenship.
