## Q1. Neighbourhood comparison: the 4–5 best-fit areas for this family, plus areas to avoid

### Takeaway
2026 price data (idealista, July–August 2026) confirm that the **Playa de San Juan–El Cabo** zone (Playa de San Juan + Cabo de las Huertas) is Alicante's most expensive, most sought-after residential area: sale price 3,834 €/m² against a city average of about 2,705–2,721 €/m². The **Zona Norte** (Virgen del Remedio–Juan XXIII, Los Ángeles–Tómbola–San Nicolás) is the cheapest, which is consistent with the brief's "areas to avoid". Provisional shortlist, based on inference and ranked by fit for this family: **(1) Playa de San Juan – PAU 5 / Condomina new-build complexes; (2) Cabo de las Huertas / Albufereta; (3) El Campello – Playa Muchavista; (4) Vistahermosa / Garbinet; (5) Sant Joan d'Alacant**. **Ensanche-Diputación** is the car-free urban alternative. I could not verify each area's atmosphere, schools and safety in this session; see Gaps.

## Q2. Realistic 2026 long-term rent for a modern 70–90 m² 2-bedroom (with AC), by area, including the premium for pool, garage and sea view

### Takeaway
- **City-wide (idealista, August 2026):** **13.9 €/m²/month (+7.3% year on year)**, i.e. about **975 € for 70 m², 1,110 € for 80 m² and 1,250 € for 90 m²**. That is the average asking price across all stock, not modern stock.
- **A good modern 2-bed with AC realistically costs about 1,100–1,400 €** in central and inland districts, and **about 1,350–1,900 €** in Playa de San Juan (PAU 5) complexes with pool and garage. One sourced 78 m² unit with pool and parking asks 1,800 €.
- **Nearby towns are cheaper:** Sant Joan d'Alacant averages 12.1 €/m²; El Campello–Muchavista is **14.5 €/m²**, about the Alicante average.

### Inferences
- **Arithmetic from the idealista city average of 13.9 €/m² (Aug 2026):** 70 m² ≈ 973 €, 80 m² ≈ 1,112 €, 90 m² ≈ 1,251 €. **Assumption, not sourced:** a modern-stock premium of +10–25% (post-2005 buildings, AC, lift, often a community pool) puts a good 80 m² flat at about **1,220–1,390 €**, before any sea view or beach location.
- **Cumulative growth:** about **10.6 €/m² in October 2023 → 12.1 (Oct 2024) → ~12.4 (Jun 2025) → 13.6 (Jun 2026) → 13.9 (Aug 2026)**. That is roughly **+31% in under three years**. At the current +7–9% a year, a 1,500 € rent in 2026 would be about 1,600–1,650 € for a new contract in 2027. Sitting tenants' annual updates are limited by the legal index (Q3).
- **Portal levels differ:** fotocasa's July 2025 level (14.14 €/m²) was already above idealista's June 2026 level (13.6 €/m²). Fotocasa and E&V figures run **~5–15% above idealista**. The report should say which portal each figure comes from and not mix them.

**Legal framework (LAU as amended by RDL 7/2019 and Ley 12/2023).** *Status of these facts: the content comes from my prior knowledge of the statutes. The URLs are the official BOE consolidated texts but were not fetched in this session.*
- **Fianza:** exactly **1 month** for a habitual-home lease. It is **2 months** for "uso distinto de vivienda", which includes seasonal leases (art. 36.1 LAU).
- **Garantía adicional:** capped at **2 months' rent** for home leases of up to 5 years, or 7 years if the landlord is a company (art. 36.5 LAU).
- **Advance rent:** the landlord **may not demand more than one month's rent in advance** (art. 17.2 LAU).
- **Agency and formalisation fees:** paid **by the landlord** (art. 20.1 LAU, as amended by Ley 12/2023).
- **Minimum duration:** 5 years, or 7 if the landlord is a legal person, through mandatory annual extensions at the tenant's option (art. 9). Then up to 3 more years of tacit extension (art. 10).
- **Seasonal leases** (art. 3 LAU) have none of the 5-year protection.
- Sources: [LAU consolidated text, BOE-A-1994-26003](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1994-26003); [Ley 12/2023, BOE-A-2023-12203](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2023-12203).

### Inferences
- **Why long-term supply is thin at the beach:** the moratorium stops *new* tourist licences, but owners in Playa de San Juan still maximise income by letting to students or remote workers from September to June and then as holiday lets or seasonal rentals in summer. At 7,500 € for August, versus 1,300–2,800 € a month for the school year, a 12-month tenancy is less attractive to them. **Search specifically for "larga duración" / "vivienda habitual" contracts. Be wary of "contratos de temporada" presented as long-term:** they give no 5-year security and carry a 2-month fianza.
- **Upfront cash, legal maximum, for a 1,500 €/month home lease:** fianza 1,500 € + additional guarantee up to 3,000 € + first month 1,500 € = **up to ~6,000 €**. No agency fee should be charged to the tenant.
- **Expected extra demands** for applicants without a Spanish payroll (inference plus common practice, **unverified**): a bank guarantee (*aval bancario*) in place of or on top of the deposit; approval by a *seguro de impago* (rent-default insurer), which usually requires Spanish income or tax returns; or informal requests for 6–12 months paid in advance. Art. 17.2 LAU says the landlord cannot *demand* more than one month in advance, but voluntary prepayment offers are common in competitive markets.
- **Budget recommendation:** have **3–6 months of rent in liquid funds**, plus proof of income: client contracts, bank statements and, ideally, Spanish tax residency or autónomo registration.
