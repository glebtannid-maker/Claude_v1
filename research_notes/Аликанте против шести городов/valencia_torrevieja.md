## 1. Valencia: international/British/American schools for age 4–5, 2026/27 fees and location

### Takeaway
Valencia has at least five British or American schools with published 2026/27 fees in a tight band. Tuition for the youngest years is **≈€6,600–7,800 a year**. With lunch, annual re-registration and similar charges, a realistic running cost is **≈€9,000–10,500 a year (≈€750–875/month spread over 12 months)**. That is about the same as King's College Alicante (€9,100 with lunch). **One-off entry fees are much higher than in Alicante:** €360 at Cambridge House, €900 at Caxton, €3,250 at BSV, and €5,200+ at ASV (capital fee, share and enrolment). Most schools sit in northern or north-western suburbs (Puçol, Rocafort, Paterna–La Cañada, Bétera). **BSV** is the main option for a family living in the city without a car.

### Cited Findings
- **British School of Valencia (BSV):**
  - Tuition **€7,150–9,750/yr for 2026/27**, paid in 10 monthly instalments — [doris.school](https://www.doris.school/schools/spain/british-school-of-valencia-main-campus) (already in the earlier notes).
  - **Admission fee €3,250**, or €1,600 for siblings. It can be split into €1,250 to confirm the place and €2,000 in April.
  - Lunch **€210/month**, compulsory from Pre-Nursery to Year 3.
  - Bus €199/month; early and late supervision €25–50/month each; music programme €790/yr.
  - Source for the fees above: [BSV School fees](https://www.bsvalencia.com/school-fees/).
  - Annual re-registration €445 (earlier notes, same page).
- **Caxton College (Puçol, north of Valencia):**
  - Tuition **€6,610–9,600/yr for 2026/27**; one-off **enrolment fee €900** for new students.
  - Baby Caxton / Nursery 1 costs €661/month (€6,610/yr).
  - Dining is listed as **included from Nursery & Reception upward**.

  - Sources: [ISDB 2026/27](https://www.international-schools-database.com/in/valencia-spain/caxton-college-valencia/fees); [doris](https://www.doris.school/schools/spain/caxton-college); official PDF "School fees Nursery to Year 13, Academic Year 2026/2027" ([caxtoncollege.com PDF](https://caxtoncollege.com/storage/school-fees-nu-y13-annual-26-27-1777389215unZ3X.pdf), not readable because the domain is blocked).

## 2. Valencia: 2026 rents for a modern 2-bed of 70–90 m² in family districts

### Takeaway
According to idealista (Aug 2026), the city average is **16.3 €/m²**, up **+3.2–3.5% year on year** and just below June's record of 16.4. That is **≈17% above Alicante** (13.9 €/m²), although Valencia rents are now rising more slowly (Alicante +7.3%). Asking prices in the target districts in Aug 2026:
- Campanar: **17.4 €/m²**
- Pla del Real: **16.4 €/m²**
- Quatre Carreres: **15.7 €/m²**
- Algirós: 15.0 €/m²
- Benimaclet: 14.8 €/m²

A **modern 2-bed with pool and/or garage realistically costs €1,500–1,900/month**. Sourced anchors: a new-build 2-bed with garage near the City of Arts and Sciences asks €1,800; long-term Patacona lets start at about €1,400; the UPV observatory's Q1 2026 average rent is about €1,700.

## 3. Valencia: car need, safety, creative/tech scene and coworking

### Takeaway
**Car:** not needed if the family lives in the city and uses BSV. Parents under 31 get **unlimited EMT/Metrovalencia travel for about €12.50/month each (EMT Jove)**, and **children up to 14 travel free**. A car becomes practically necessary with a suburban school (Puçol, Bétera, La Cañada) unless the school bus is used.

**Safety:** Valencia city had **74.2 criminal offences per 1,000 inhabitants in 2025**, about **double Alicante's 36.2** and above Spain's ≈50. The level was flat year on year (−0.3%). Offences are dominated by theft and cybercrime; violent-crime detail was not retrieved.

**Scene:** Valencia is a genuine tech and startup hub: Marina de Empresas/Lanzadera, **≈50 coworkings**, hot desks at €150–250/month. It is clearly stronger than Alicante for a young freelance creative.

### Cited Findings
- **Public transport 2026:**
  - Children up to 14 travel free on Generalitat transport. On Metrovalencia, under-10s travel free with an adult, up to 2 per ticket.
  - Under-31s get **50% off youth titles** (Móbilis30), including residents with legal residence in Spain.
  - Discounts of 40% or more on passes and multi-trip tickets are extended to the end of 2026.
  - **EMT Jove costs €12.50/month** for unlimited trips for under-30s.
  - Sources: [Metrovalencia – Generalitat mantiene precios 2026](https://www.metrovalencia.es/es/la-generalitat-mantiene-en-2026-los-precios-del-transporte-publico-autonomico-y-la-gratuidad-para-los-afectados-por-la-dana-y-menores-de-hasta-14-anos/); [Valencia Extra](https://www.valenciaextra.com/es/valencia/metrovalencia-mantendra-tarifas-reducidas-hasta-finales-2026-consulta-quien-se-beneficia_590640_102.html); [Metrovalencia Móbilis30](https://www.metrovalencia.es/es/mobilis30-suma-mensual-jove/); [El Español, 26 Dec 2025](https://www.elespanol.com/valencia/20251226/oficial-transporte-publico-valencia-costara-mitad-gratis-usuarios-trt/1003744070329_0.html).
  - *Uncertain:* whether the EMT Jove age limit is under 30 or under 31.
- **Crime:**
  - 2025 crime index for Valencia city: **74.2 offences per 1,000 inhabitants**. **Residential burglaries: 1,584** (1.88 per 1,000). Full year: **62,484 offences (−0.3%)**. Conventional crime fell 4.6% in Jan–Sep 2025, against −0.6% nationally. The national conventional-crime rate is 40.8 per 1,000. Sources: [El Español Valencia, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.elespanol.com/valencia/20251208/valencia-reduce-delincuencia-supera-madrid-barcelona/1003744046762_0.html); [Interior Q4-2025 balance](https://www.interior.gob.es/opencms/export/sites/default/.galleries/galeria-de-prensa/documentos-y-multimedia/balances-e-informes/2025/Balance-de-Criminalidad_Cuarto_Trimestre_2025.pdf).
  - *Note:* the 74.2 figure's exact origin (press calculation or portal) is not visible. However, 62,484 ÷ 74.2 × 1,000 ≈ 842,000 people, which is consistent with Valencia's population.
  - Alicante for comparison: **13,258 offences in 2025 = 36.2 per 1,000** (30.1 excluding cybercrime); +12.2% in 2025 and +7.8% in H1 2026 — from `city_international_russian.md` §3 ([El Debate, 19 Mar 2026](https://www.eldebate.com/espana/comunidad-valenciana/20260319/mapa-criminalidad-alicante-estos-son-delitos-han-disparado-ultimo-ano_397536.html)).
  - Neighbourhood-level crime in Valencia (secondary source): [Selectra](https://selectra.es/alarmas/barrios-peligrosos/valencia) (not read).
- **Tech and coworking scene:**
  - About **50 coworking spaces**, including Wayco, Lanzadera and The Shed; hot desks at **€150–250/month** — [ValenciaMove coworking 2026](https://valenciamove.com/coworking-spaces-valencia/); [nomadcowork](https://nomadcowork.com/spaces/countries/spain/valencia/) (secondary blogs).
  - **Lanzadera** was founded in 2013 by Juan Roig at Marina de Empresas, alongside EDEM business school, Angels, the Insomnia accelerator, Biohub and Sesame. It has accelerated **over 1,000 startups** — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/valencia-tech-sector-startup-ecosystem-poised-to-explode); [Startup Valencia](https://startupvalencia.org/tech-ecosystem/).
  - Startup investment rose from €58.5m (2021) to €73m (2022). The Valencian region has the most startups per capita in Spain. These are older figures quoted by TNW and Startup Valencia.

### Inferences
- **Transport budget:** with EMT Jove at 2 × €12.50 and the child free, Valencia without a car costs less than Alicante's €220 line. The budget uses **€170** (E: passes, taxis to the airport and at night, occasional car rental). A family with a suburban school should budget either a car (≈€540/month full cost, same as Alicante's used hybrid) or a school bus at ≈€199/month (BSV's price as a proxy).
- **Safety:** the per-capita rate is roughly twice Alicante's, and the difference is mostly large-city property crime and tourist-area pickpocketing. For a family in Campanar, Pla del Real or Patacona the practical risk is still low, but **on the statistics Valencia loses to Alicante on safety**.
- **Creative and tech scene: a clear win for Valencia.** It offers a larger freelance/motion-design client and peer network, meetups and coworkings. The Alicante equivalent is Distrito Digital, which is smaller (per the earlier notes; not quantified).

### Gaps
- 2026 H1 Interior figures for Valencia city; district crime data; motion-design- or animation-specific communities (ESAT, Weird Market; unverified leads from the earlier notes). Out of search budget.

## 4. Torrevieja: British/international schools reachable for age 4–5, and fees

### Takeaway
There is **no British school inside Torrevieja** in the results. Three are nearby:
- **El Limonar International School Villamartín (ELIS)**, Villamartín/San Miguel de Salinas: the strongest brand. 2025/26 first-year cost for a 3-year-old ≈**€8,350** including the €1,500 registration fee; lunch €1,800/yr is compulsory. Recurring cost ≈**€7,000–9,000/yr (E)**.

- **Willow International School**, Doña Pepa, Ciudad Quesada: ages 4–16; fees €5,250–6,000/yr, but only a 2022/23 figure was found.

Registration is **€1,500** (non-refundable) and lunch **€1,800/yr** (compulsory to Year 11). Sibling discounts are 10/20/40%. Sources: [ISDB 2025/26](https://www.international-schools-database.com/in/alicante-costa-blanca/el-limonar-international-school-villamartin-costa-blanca/fees); [ELIS fees](https://elisvillamartin.com/fees/).

## 5. Torrevieja: 2026 rents for a modern 2-bed, car dependence and off-season life

### Takeaway
idealista puts Torrevieja at **12.4 €/m²** in July 2026, **+4.1% year on year** (11.9 in July 2025). That is **≈11% below Alicante** (13.9) and **24% below Valencia** (16.3).

Headline 2-bed listings start at **€700–800/month plus bills**, but many are **winter lets (Sep/Oct–May/Jun)**. For a modern 2-bed with pool and garage on a **12-month contract**, **≈€1,000–1,250** is realistic (E); the budget uses **€1,100**.

There is **no railway**. There are direct buses to Alicante airport (≈50–60 min, €7, 12–19 a day), but daily life with a child at a school in Villamartín or Quesada **needs a car**.

### Cited Findings
- **Rents:**
  - 12.4 €/m² in July 2026, +4.1% year on year (earlier notes, [idealista](https://www.idealista.com/sala-de-prensa/informes-precio-vivienda/alquiler/comunitat-valenciana/alicante-alacant/torrevieja/)). A July 2025 value of 11.9 €/m² appears in the same series — [idealista](https://www.idealista.com/sala-de-prensa/informes-precio-vivienda/alquiler/comunitat-valenciana/alicante-alacant/torrevieja/).   - Caveats: the large non-registered seasonal population inflates any per-resident rate. INE's official population was not retrieved.
  - Even so, **Torrevieja loses to Alicante on safety statistics**, and the trend was rising through 2025.
- **Rent:** the €700–800 headline prices are traps for a family. Winter lets end in May or June, forcing a move or summer prices. The family should insist on a 12-month *vivienda habitual* contract (LAU), which in this market probably costs **€1,000–1,250 for a modern 2-bed with pool and garage** (E, extrapolated from 12.4 €/m² × 80 m² ≈ €990 plus a modern premium). Saving versus Alicante's €1,500: **≈€300–500/month**.

Costs as in Alicante's used-hybrid model: **€538/month full cost** (€368 cash). Garages are often included in rentals, so that part could be lower. A second car may be wanted if the mother needs mobility while the father works (not budgeted).

For a couple of 24 the off-season social life is thin. The international mix is mostly Ukrainian and Russian (≈16.5 thousand combined), Eastern European, Latin American and northern-European retirees, per the earlier notes. That is the **"Russian bubble" risk** the family wants to avoid. No 2026 data on events or nightlife were gathered.

## 7. Where Valencia and Torrevieja beat or lose to Alicante (summary for the report)

### Takeaway
**Valencia** is the better city for a young creative family: tech and creative scene, coworkings, metro and tram, EMT Jove at €12.50/month, more school choice, and the child can go car-free with BSV. It costs **≈$270/month more** at the same standard, has higher entry fees, and statistically **twice Alicante's crime rate**.

**Torrevieja** is cheaper on rent (−€300 to −€500/month), but the compulsory car cancels most of the saving (net ≈$140/month cheaper than car-free Alicante). It has thin school choice (ELIS as the only strong British option), a record crime level in 2025 (≈90+ per 1,000), seasonal rentals and off-season life, and a large Russian/Ukrainian-speaking population (bubble risk). **For this family Torrevieja is a weaker choice than Alicante. Valencia is the only real alternative, and only at ≥$7–8k income.**
