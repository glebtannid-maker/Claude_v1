## 1. Residence route: Italy digital nomad / remote worker visa, and the path to long-term status and citizenship

### Takeaway
The legal route exists and is cheap in income terms. The threshold is about **€28,000 gross/year**, plus an extra amount per family member (cited as about €780/month). The route is open to freelancers, and the spouse and child can follow on family permits. **The critical risk for this family is the "highly qualified" requirement.** Without a university degree, the father must show **about 5 years of relevant professional experience**. The 3-year ICT shortcut does not fit a motion designer. A 24-year-old can meet 5 years only if his freelance and design work since about age 19 is well documented. The permit is **1 year, renewable**. Russians must apply either at the Italian consulate or visa centre in Moscow or, possibly, at the Italian embassy in Jakarta on the strength of a KITAS. Jakarta practice is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis.** Ministry of Interior decree of **29 Feb 2024**, published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale n. 79 on **4 Apr 2024**. It sets the entry and permit procedure for non-EU nationals who do "highly qualified work" remotely using technology — [GU text (PDF, via Piemonte Immigrazione)](https://www.piemonteimmigrazione.it/images/news_materiali/Decreto_Nomadi_digitali.pdf); [Wikilabour](https://www.wikilabour.it/segnalazioni/rapporto-di-lavoro/ministero-dellinterno-decreto-29-febbraio-2024/); [FISCOeTASSE](https://www.fiscoetasse.com/normativa-prassi/13348-ingresso-lavoratori-nomadi-digitali-decreto-in-gu.html).
- **Income.** At least **3× the minimum income for exemption from healthcare co-payments**, i.e. **about €28,000/year**, backed by financial or bank documents proving continuity and amount. The same requirements list **6 months' prior experience as a digital nomad/remote worker**, a work or collaboration contract (or binding offer) for highly qualified work, health insurance, and proof of accommodation — [same decree sources](https://www.fiscoetasse.com/normativa-prassi/13348-ingresso-lavoratori-nomadi-digitali-decreto-in-gu.html); [Consolato Generale New York DN page](https://consnewyork.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-straniero/visti/visas-to-enter-italy/digital-nomad-remote-worker-visa/).
- **Extra income per family member.** "An extra monthly income of **€780 per family member**" — [GlobalCitizenSolutions (secondary, 2026)](https://www.globalcitizensolutions.com/italy-digital-nomad-visa/). Not verified against a consular source (see Gaps).
- **Degree or experience.** Without a degree, applicants need "**at least 5 years of professional experience** in their field or **3 years in specialized ICT roles**" — [GlobalCitizenSolutions / aggregator summary](https://www.globalcitizensolutions.com/italy-digital-nomad-visa/). The decree ties "highly qualified" to the EU Blue Card definition in art. 27-quater TUI. The Blue Card guide wording is "qualifica professionale superiore attestata da almeno tre anni di esperienza professionale pertinente acquisita nei sette anni precedenti" (this is the ICT/managers variant) — [Infoimmigrazione Carta Blu 2026](https://www.infoimmigrazione.com/carta-blu-ue/); [Min. Interno DLCI Carta Blu](https://libertaciviliimmigrazione.dlci.interno.gov.it/la-carta-blu-ue-art-27quater-dlgs-28698-ingresso-lavoratori-altamente-qualificati). **Conflict:** secondary sources disagree on whether the non-ICT experience route is 3 or 5 years; see Gaps.
- **Who can apply.** Both self-employed freelancers ("digital nomads") and employees or contractors of foreign companies ("remote workers") are covered, and both must be "highly specialized" — [GlobalCitizenSolutions](https://www.globalcitizensolutions.com/italy-digital-nomad-visa/).
- **Health insurance.** The visa requires a private policy covering medical treatment and hospitalisation for the whole stay. A minimum of **€30,000** is cited by secondary sources — [GlobalCitizenSolutions](https://www.globalcitizensolutions.com/italy-digital-nomad-visa/); [Portale Immigrazione](https://portaleimmigrazione.eu/permesso-di-soggiorno-nomadi-digitali-e-lavoratori-da-remoto/).
- **Permit length and family.** The permit lasts **1 year and is renewable annually** while requirements are met. **Family reunification** with art. 29 TUI relatives (spouse, minor children) is allowed, and family members get a family-reasons permit of the same duration — [avv. Lombardini](https://www.avvocatofrancescolombardini.it/visto-e-permesso-di-soggiorno-per-nomadi-digitali/); [Infoimmigrazione 2026](https://www.infoimmigrazione.com/visto-per-nomadi-digitali/); [Portale Immigrazione](https://portaleimmigrazione.eu/permesso-di-soggiorno-nomadi-digitali-e-lavoratori-da-remoto/).
- **Tax registration.** Self-employed holders must open a **partita IVA** and register with INPS Gestione Separata — [Infoimmigrazione](https://www.infoimmigrazione.com/visto-per-nomadi-digitali/).
- **Where Russians apply.** At the Italian consulate competent for their registered residence. In Russia that is the **Consulate General in Moscow** (10 Yakimanskaya nab.) or the **VMS visa centre** (Maly Tolmachevsky 6/1), which has a dedicated "visa for digital nomads" document page. Type D fee **€116**; processing **15–45 working days** — [Italy VMS – DN visa (Moscow)](https://italyvms.com/documents-for-moscow/visa-for-digital-nomads/); [italyvms.com](https://italyvms.com/en/).
- **Jakarta.** The Italian Embassy in Jakarta (Jl. Diponegoro 45) takes visa applications by appointment only, via visa.jakarta@esteri.it, and there is a VFS national-visa form for Indonesia — [Ambasciata d'Italia Jakarta – contacts](https://ambjakarta.esteri.it/en/chi-siamo/contatti/); [VFS national visa form (PDF)](https://www.vfsglobal.com/Italy/indonesia/pdf/New_National_Visa_Form.pdf). Whether Jakarta accepts a DN application from a Russian with a Bali KITAS was **not confirmed**.
- **EU long-term residence (general rule).** It requires 5 years of legal, continuous residence. For Blue Card holders, absences under 12 consecutive months and 18 months in total do not break continuity — [Programma Integra – Carta blu](https://www.programmaintegra.it/wp/risorse/schede-tematiche/il-permesso-di-soggiorno/il-permesso-di-soggiorno-carta-blu-ue/); [italianvisa.it](https://italianvisa.it/it/accesso-agevolato-lungo-periodo/).
- **Citizenship.**
  - The **8–9 June 2025 referendum** proposed cutting the residence requirement for naturalisation from **10 to 5 years**. Turnout was **30.58%**, far below the 50%+1 quorum, so it failed. The citizenship question itself got 65% "yes". **The 10-year rule remains** — [Wikipedia 2025 Italian referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_referendum); [Quotidiano.net results](https://www.quotidiano.net/politica/risultati-referendum-2025-diretta-affluenza-mg31sdc6); [Min. Interno dossier](https://dait.interno.gov.it/documenti/dossier-referendum2025.pdf).
  - The 2025 law change (DL 36/2025 → L. 74/2025) restricted citizenship *by descent* (iure sanguinis). It does not change naturalisation by residence. This is researcher knowledge and was not re-verified in this session; see Gaps.
- **Schengen rules for Russians.** Commission Implementing Decision **C(2025) 7552 of 6 Nov 2025** (announced 7 Nov 2025) ended routine multiple-entry Schengen visas for Russians. **Old rule:** multiple-entry visas under the Visa Code cascade (already restricted since 2022). **New rule:** Russians generally get **single-entry** visas, with exceptions, including multiple-entry visas of up to 1 year for close family of Russians *residing in the EU* — [European Commission, Migration & Home Affairs](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/commission-implementing-decision-establishing-rules-issuing-multiple-entry-visas-russian-nationals_en); [Decision text PDF](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/409a8179-9885-49f8-ab40-9c1a07b1f581_en); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/schengen-visas-european-commission-tightens-rules-on-multiple-entry-visas-for-russian-nationals). This affects short-stay C visas (for example, visiting relatives coming to Italy), not the national D visa or an Italian residence permit.

### Inferences
- **Degree or experience.** If the father has no degree, the application depends on proving about 5 years of relevant experience (the safe reading). Useful evidence: contracts, invoices, client references, portfolio, and tax or bank records since about 2021. At 24 this is borderline. A diploma or certificates in design or animation would help. A consulate rejection is a real risk, and consular practice varies.
- **Income test.** $60k ≈ €52.8k, comfortably above €28k plus family add-ons (about €28k + 2 × €9.4k ≈ €46.7k if the €780/month per member figure is right). $60k passes, but with less headroom than it seems.
- **Where to apply.** Applying from Moscow is the most predictable route: the VMS has a dedicated DN checklist. Applying from Indonesia is plausible only if Jakarta accepts KITAS holders; this must be confirmed by email.
- **Renewal and absences.** Renewal is yearly. Long absences (Bali, Russia) risk both renewal and the 5-year continuity needed for EU long-term status. Whether DN permit years count toward the EU long-term permit (art. 9 TUI) was not verified; general practice suggests renewable work-type permits count, but this is unconfirmed.
- **Banking and sanctions.** Not researched here. Use the Alicante notes (dnv_residence_russians.md): EU Reg. 833/2014 art. 5b sets a €100k deposit cap for Russian nationals who are not EU residents; this does not apply once they hold an Italian residence permit. The same logic applies in Italy.

- Whether the DN permit counts toward the EU long-term residence permit (art. 9 TUI), and whether it can be converted to a "lavoro autonomo" permit in-country: no direct source found.
- Jakarta embassy acceptance of Russian KITAS holders for DN visas: unconfirmed.
- L. 74/2025 details: not re-verified in this session.

## 2. Taxes for a partita IVA freelancer in Turin (2026) and computed burden at $60k / $72k / $96k

### Takeaway
- **Most likely regime: forfettario at 15%.** The 5% start-up rate is very likely unavailable, because moving an existing freelance business to Italy counts as "mere continuation" of a prior activity, and the 3 prior years count even if the activity was abroad.
- **Burden under forfettario 15%:** about **29% of gross**, INPS included, at every income level.
- **Impatriati alternative:** ordinary IRPEF plus the impatriati regime (60% exemption with a minor child) gives about **24–27%**. But impatriati **cannot be combined with forfettario**, and its "high qualification" test without a degree is uncertain.
- **Without any incentive:** ordinary IRPEF costs **40–45%**.
- **$96k (≈ €84.5k) sits right under the €85k forfettario ceiling.** A weaker euro or a good year would push him out.

### Cited Findings
- **Forfettario rates.** Substitute tax **5% for the first 5 years** of a new activity, then **15%**. The 5% requires:
  - no artistic, professional or business activity in the **3 prior years**;
  - the new activity must not be a **mere continuation** of prior employment or self-employment.
  - **Activities carried out abroad count** for this test.
  - Continuing a foreign activity after moving to Italy **is denied the 5%**.
  - Sources: [Fiscomania – 5% start-up](https://fiscomania.com/imposta-sostitutiva-al-5-per-il-regime-forfettario/); [FISCOeTASSE – "negato il 5% se si prosegue l'attività estera trasferendola in Italia"](https://www.fiscoetasse.com/rassegna-stampa/32273-regime-forfettario-negato-il-5-se-si-prosegue-lattivita-estera-trasferendola-in-italia.html); [FidoCommercialista 2026](https://fidocommercialista.it/5-forfettario-requisiti-accesso).
- **Forfettario access.** The regime is closed to non-residents, except EU/EEA residents earning ≥75% of their income in Italy. An Italian tax resident is therefore eligible — [Fiscomania – regime forfettario 2026](https://fiscomania.com/regime-forfettario/); [finom](https://finom.co/it-it/blog/regime-forfettario-requisiti/). The €85,000 revenue ceiling and the 78% coefficient for professional activities are standard and appear in the [Flextax forfettario 2026](https://flextax.it/regime-forfettario-2026/) and [partitaiva.it 2026](https://www.partitaiva.it/regime-forfettario-2026-limiti/) search results. The exact "exit at €100k" wording was not re-read; see Gaps.
- **INPS Gestione Separata 2026.**
  - Rate **26.07%** (confirmed by INPS circular n. 8 of 3 Feb 2026, unchanged since 2024).
  - For forfettari the base is revenue × coefficient (example: €50k revenue × 78% → €10,167 INPS).
  - Ceiling (massimale) **€122,295**; minimum income for full contribution credit **€18,808**.
  - Sources: [Calcolatore Forfettario](https://calcolatoreforfettario.com/blog/aliquota-inps-gestione-separata-2026); [QuickFisco](https://quickfisco.it/blog/regime-forfettario/calcolo-gestione-separata-inps-esempi-regime-forfettario/); [forfettari.it](https://www.forfettari.it/gestione-separata-inps).
  - **Correction:** one extract said a "35% reduction to 16.95%" applies. That reduction is for artigiani/commercianti forfettari, not Gestione Separata (researcher knowledge), so it is **not applied**.
- **INPS deductibility.** INPS paid is deductible from forfettario income before the substitute tax, and from total income under ordinary IRPEF. This is standard rule (art. 1 c. 64 L. 190/2014; art. 10 TUIR), not re-read in this session. It is **applied in the model**.
- **IRPEF 2026.** Rates **23% to €28k, 33% on €28–50k, 43% above €50k**. The Legge di Bilancio 2026 (L. 199/2025, art. 1 c. 3) cut the second rate from **35% to 33% from 1 Jan 2026**, with the benefit neutralised above €200k income — [MEF – misure legge di bilancio 2026](https://www.mef.gov.it/focus/Principali-misure-della-legge-di-bilancio-2026/); [Fiscomania – aliquote IRPEF](https://fiscomania.com/aliquote-irpef/).
- **Piemonte regional surcharge 2026–2027** (progressive): **1.62%** to €15k; **2.68%** €15–28k; **3.31%** €28–50k; **3.33%** above €50k — [Regione Piemonte – addizionale regionale](https://www.regione.piemonte.it/web/temi/tributi/addizionali-regionali/addizionale-regionale-allirpef); [MEF finanze addregirpef](https://www1.finanze.gov.it/finanze2/dipartimentopolitichefiscali/fiscalitalocale/addregirpef/addregirpef.php?reg=13).
- **Turin municipal surcharge** (progressive): **0.8%** to €28k; **1.1%** €28–50k; **1.2%** above €50k; **exempt if income ≤ €11,790** (Reg. n. 322, amended by DCC n. 195 of 29 Mar 2022) — [Comune di Torino – addizionale comunale IRPEF](https://www.comune.torino.it/schede-informative/addizionale-comunale-irpef); [TuttoCalcolo Torino 2026](https://www.tuttocalcolo.it/addizionale-irpef/piemonte/torino). Surcharges do not apply under forfettario (substitute tax).
- **Impatriati regime** (art. 5 D.Lgs. 209/2023, for residence moves from tax year 2024):
  - Employment-type income and **self-employment income from arts and professions** produced in Italy count toward taxable income at only **50%**, up to **€600,000/year** — [Agenzia Entrate – Lavoratori impatriati 209/2023](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/web/guest/lavoratori-impatriati-209-2023/infogen-lavoratori-impatriati-209-2023); [UniPa summary](https://www.unipa.it/servizi/retribuzioni/regimi-fiscali-agevolati/agevolazione-fiscale-per-lavoratori-impatriati/).
  - The exemption rises to **60% with a minor child** — [Fiscomania – impatriati 2026](https://fiscomania.com/regime-impatriati-rientro-dei-cervelli/).
  - It lasts for the year residence is acquired plus the 4 following years — [Fiscomania – impatriati vs forfettario](https://fiscomania.com/lavoratori-impatriati-vs-regime-forfettario/).
  - It requires the high-qualification requirements of **D.Lgs. 108/2012 and D.Lgs. 206/2007**. Taxpayers without a degree have asked whether professional qualification alone suffices, and the Agenzia has issued rulings on this: [Risposta n. 71/2025](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/8703578/Risposta+n.+71_2025/f871902d-bef2-718c-47df-53d404d36fab); [FiscoOggi – "Cittadinanza e titolo di studio"](https://www.fiscooggi.it/portale/-/cittadinanza-e-titolo-di-studio-nuovi-chiarimenti-per-gli-impatriati-). The content of the ruling was not readable.
  - The 3-year prior non-residence and 4-year commitment conditions are the standard 2024 rules (researcher knowledge, consistent with AdE pages).
- **Impatriati and forfettario are not cumulative (verified).** Choosing forfettario rules out impatriati for the remainder of the 5-year window — [FiscoOggi – "Regime forfetario: incompatibile con quello sui lavoratori impatriati"](https://www.fiscooggi.it/portale/-/regime-forfetario-incompatibile-con-quello-sui-lavoratori-impatriati); [Consulenti del Lavoro](https://www.consulentidellavoro.it/home/storico-articoli/16450-con-il-forfetario-esclusa-la-fruizione-del-regime-per-impatriati); [AdE Circ. 33/E 2020](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/2957155/Circolare+n.+33+del+28122020.pdf/e22ac901-2a2c-e580-5516-9b2725a760b3); [Edotto](https://www.edotto.com/articolo/regime-forfetario-e-degli-impatriati-opzioni-non-sovrapponibili). **Conflict:** one Fiscomania extract claims they can be cumulated. [Il Sole 24 Ore NT+](https://ntplusdiritto.ilsole24ore.com/art/impatriati-e-forfetari-l-agenzia-apre-cambio-corsa-AFy1uFbD) reports the Agenzia "opens to a change mid-way", i.e. leaving forfettario to enter impatriati in later years. **The Agenzia's position is: not cumulative.**

### Computed tax + INPS (Python; FX 1.1367)

**Assumptions**
- *Forfettario:* taxable = revenue × 78%; INPS = 26.07% of taxable; substitute tax on (taxable − INPS), steady state; no VAT, IRAP or surcharges.
- *Ordinary:* professional income = revenue − 10% real work expenses (deductible). INPS is 26.07% of that income on the full base (the impatriati exemption does not reduce INPS). IRPEF 2026 brackets apply to (income × (1 − exemption) − INPS).
- *Credits in ordinary:* self-employment credit (art. 13 c. 5 TUIR) and the dependent-spouse credit (art. 12). No child credit (replaced by Assegno Unico). Piemonte and Torino surcharges as above.
- *Not included:* €2 stamp duty per invoice (~€25/yr), accountant fees (inside the 10% expenses), and Assegno Unico (the child benefit, ~€57+/month minimum; eligibility for non-EU requires 2 years' residence, not modelled).

### Inferences
- **Realistic base case: forfettario 15%, total ≈ 29% of gross.** The father already runs a freelance business in Bali, and moving it to Italy is "continuation", so the 5% is almost certainly lost. In practice, forfettario is also the simplest regime and needs no degree proof.
- **Impatriati 60%** beats forfettario 15% by roughly $300–370/month net at all three incomes. It needs:
  - a defensible "high qualification", which is weak without a degree;
  - 3 prior years of non-Italian residence (met);
  - staying resident ≥4 years (a 4-year clawback risk if they leave early);
  - ordinary bookkeeping (a costlier accountant).
- **Decision point.** Once forfettario is chosen in year 1, impatriati is blocked for that window per the AdE. Year 1 is therefore the moment to get a tax adviser's ruling (interpello) on the qualification test.
- **The $96k ceiling problem.** $96k = €84.5k, only €545 below the €85k ceiling. A 1% EUR weakening, or any extra invoice, crosses it and triggers exit from forfettario the following year (researcher knowledge: immediate exit only above €100k). **At $8k/month, plan for the ordinary regime** (with or without impatriati) from year 2.
- **Comparison with Spain.** Under forfettario 15%, Italy is roughly on a par with or cheaper than Spanish autónomo + IRPF at these incomes. Under ordinary IRPEF without incentives, Italy is clearly more expensive (about 40–45%).

- The €85k / €100k exit mechanics and the 78% coefficient for ATECO 74.10 (design) / 59.1x (video production) were not re-read from a 2026 primary source. The 78% coefficient is standard for "attività professionali, scientifiche, tecniche"; 59.xx production would be 67% or 78% depending on the code. **Verify the ATECO code.**
- The content of AdE Risposta 71/2025 on the no-degree impatriati qualification: unreadable.
- Assegno Unico 2026 amounts and non-EU eligibility for DN permit holders: not searched.
- The model is steady state. In year 1, INPS advances cause cash-flow humps.

## 3. International and English-language preschools for a 4–5-year-old in Turin (2026/27)

### Takeaway
**The International School of Turin (IST, IB PYP, ages 3–18) is the default choice.** 2026/27 tuition: **Pre-K €8,300**, **Kindergarten €10,200**. Add a one-off **registration fee of €3,300 (€3,800 after the early deadline)**, lunch of about **€1,100/yr** and an optional bus of about **€1,400/yr**. First-year cost ≈ **€14,600–16,000**; ongoing ≈ **€11,300/yr** with lunch.

IST is in the **eastern hills (Strada Pecetto 34, Chieri/Pecetto area)**, **not in Vinovo/Moncalieri**, so housing in the Precollina/Collina or the eastern centre makes sense. Alternatives:
- **WINS**, Via Traves 28 (north-west Turin), fees from about €10.5k, not published;
- several **bilingual Italian paritarie** (Saint Denis, BEST/Poppy, San Massimo, Pingu's), which are cheaper but Italian-majority.

### Cited Findings
- **IST 2026/27 tuition** (per doris.school, labelled 2026/27): **Nursery €8,300; Pre-Kindergarten €8,300; Kindergarten €10,200; Grade 1 €13,200**. ## 4. Housing: family neighbourhoods and 2026 rents for a modern 2-bedroom (70–90 m²)

### Takeaway
- **Turin's average asking rent hit a record 13.1 €/m² in August 2026** (idealista, +1.8% y/y). That is **below Alicante's 13.9 €/m²** and rising more slowly (Alicante +7.3% y/y).
- **At the city average:** 70 m² ≈ €917, 80 m² ≈ €1,048, 90 m² ≈ €1,179.
- **Crocetta** (elegant, near the Politecnico and Corso Trento schools) runs about 12.7 €/m²; **San Salvario** about 13.3 €/m²; **Vanchiglia** 12–15 €/m².

## 6. Brief city facts: population, community, safety, air, climate, geography, creative scene, connectivity

### Takeaway
- **Turin is a big, calm, industrial-heritage city** of about 860k people, 16% of them foreigners. Mostly Romanian, Moroccan and Peruvian, **not an expat hub**.
- **English is used less** in daily life than in Alicante or Lisbon.
- **Russian infrastructure is small** but present: a Saturday school and cultural associations, and no bubble.
- **Minuses:** Po Valley air (PM10 limit exceeded on 39 days in 2025), foggy grey winters, humid summers, and crime statistics in the upper tier of Italian provinces.
- **Pluses:** the Alps about 1 hour away and the Ligurian sea 1h30–2h; a strong animation/VFX/design niche (VIEW Conference, Torino Film Festival); Frecciarossa to Milan in about 50 min and to Paris in about 5h40.

### Inferences
- **Climate (researcher knowledge, not sourced this session).** Continental, humid subtropical fringe:
  - winters about 0–8 °C with fog and grey inversions from Nov to Feb;
  - summers about 28–32 °C and humid;
  - about 900 mm of rain a year.
  
  This is a big contrast with Alicante's roughly 300 sunny days and mild winters. For a family coming from Bali, **winter is the biggest lifestyle downgrade**.
- **Geography (knowledge).** Ski resorts of the Via Lattea (Sestriere, Sauze d'Oulx, Bardonecchia) are about 1–1.5 h away. Liguria (Savona, Varazze, Alassio) is about 1.5–2 h by car or train. Milan is about 50 min by Frecciarossa.

## 7. Assessment: where Turin beats and loses to Alicante

### Takeaway
**Turin wins on:**
- creative-industry fit (VIEW Conference, animation/VFX, design);
- city scale and culture;
- rail links (Milan in 50 min, Paris by direct train) and Alps access;
- lower rent per m² with a less speculative rental market;
- potentially a lower tax burden, *if* the family can use forfettario (29%) or impatriati.

**Turin loses on:**
- climate (fog, grey winters, humid summers) and air quality (Po Valley);
- no beach on the doorstep;
- a smaller, less English-speaking international community;
- more expensive and farther-out international schooling (IST €10.2k + €3.3k registration, in the hills; a car is effectively needed);
- higher total living cost (about €700–850/month more);
- a stricter "highly qualified" DN test for a young applicant without a degree.

**For this family, the net income at $5–6k is far below the Turin comfort budget.** Turin is only realistic at **$8k+** with a lean-comfort standard, or at about $11–12k for full comfort.
