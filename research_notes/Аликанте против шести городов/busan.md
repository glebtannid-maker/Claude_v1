## 1. Residence route: F-1-D "Workation / Digital Nomad" visa

### Takeaway
The preliminary facts are **confirmed**: Ministry of Justice press release dated 7 Jul 2026 says the F-1-D became a regular (non-pilot) visa from **30 Jun 2026**, maximum stay rose **2 → 3 years**, income test is now **1×–2× prior-year GNI per capita**, and applicants **aged 18–34 who live outside Seoul/Incheon/Gyeonggi need only 1× GNI (~KRW 52.4M ≈ $38.6k)**. The father (24) qualifies on income even at $5k/month. The **real blockers are (a) the "employee of a foreign company or owner of a foreign business, ≥1 year in the same field" test — pure freelancers are not clearly eligible — and (b) F-1-D gives no route to F-2/F-5 permanent residence.**

### Cited Findings
**Official status / dates (old vs new rule)**
- MOJ press release, 7 Jul 2026 (title: "'연봉 1억' 문턱은 낮추고 체류기간은 최대 3년으로…"): digital nomad (workation) visa, piloted since 2024, **officially operated from 30 Jun 2026** — [MOJ/Immigration press release PDF, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/moj/182/496858/download.do); news: [Nate/Yonhap-type report 7 Jul 2026 "법무부, '디지털 노마드' 비자 정식 운영…체류 기간↑·소득 요건↓"](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260707n31747)
- **Max stay: old rule** 1 year on registration + 1-year extension = max 2 years (pilot 1 Jan 2024 – 31 Dec 2025, then continued until the permanent launch); **new rule from 30 Jun 2026: up to 3 years**, justified in the release as letting talent "experience and choose Korea as a settlement destination" — [MOJ press release 7 Jul 2026 (search extract)](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/moj/182/496858/download.do); old rule text: [Weekly Korean NZ summary of embassy notice](https://www.weeklykoreanz.com/post/%EC%9B%8C%EC%BC%80%EC%9D%B4%EC%85%98-%EB%94%94%EC%A7%80%ED%84%B8%EB%85%B8%EB%A7%88%EB%93%9C-f-1-d-%EB%B9%84%EC%9E%90-%EC%8B%A0%EC%B2%AD-%EC%95%88%EB%82%B4)
- Pilot result: **743 F-1-D visas issued Jan 2024 – May 2026** (2 years 5 months) — [MOJ press release 7 Jul 2026](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/moj/182/496858/download.do). (Very small numbers → consulates have little routine experience.)
- **Income, old rule:** ≥2× previous-year GNI per capita (Bank of Korea) for everyone — [Korean Embassy Canada F-1-D page](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/ca-ko/brd/m_5346/view.do?seq=1189450); one secondary source says "after tax deduction" (~$75,400/yr) — [digitalnomadskorea.com / aggregated search extract](https://www.digitalnomadskorea.com/post/everything-you-need-to-know-about-koreas-digital-nomad-visa) (UNCERTAIN whether gross or net is required).
- **Income, new rule (from 30 Jun 2026):** 1×–2× GNI depending on age and region; **18–34 year-olds doing workation outside the capital region → 1× GNI** — [MOJ press release 7 Jul 2026](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/moj/182/496858/download.do). Standard 2× ≈ **KRW 104.8M**; 1× ≈ **KRW 52.4M** (2025 GNI per capita ≈ KRW 52.416M) — [Freaking Nomads, 2026](https://freakingnomads.com/news/south-korea-digital-nomad-visa-permanent-lower-thresholds-2026/); [Korean-language search summary citing 5,241.6만원](https://www.bangduty.com/en/report/korea-workation-visa-f1-d). Freaking Nomads converts 1× to ~US$36,963 (at their FX); at 1,356 = **~$38,650**; 2× = **~$77,300**.
- Separate May 2026 measure: visa-free workation on **Jeju** extended to 90 days — [Nate news 4 May 2026](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260504n23556) (not relevant for Busan).

**Who qualifies (freelancer question)**
- Official wording: "foreigner **owning an overseas business or belonging to an overseas company**, able to work remotely, who has worked **≥1 year in the same industry**, and their family" — [Korean Embassy Canada](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/ca-ko/brd/m_5346/view.do?seq=1189450); [Korean Embassy Ireland](https://www.mofa.go.kr/ie-ko/brd/m_8187/view.do?seq=1251168).
- Secondary guides: "Freelancers who do not have an overseas employer or business affiliation should not assume that they are eligible. For example, the **Korean Embassy in Singapore explicitly states that freelancers are not eligible**"; self-employed "through a registered overseas business" may fall under the business-owner category; clients must be outside Korea; business must be registered outside Korea — [Greenback / A Way Abroad / Expert Sapiens search extracts](https://www.greenbacktaxservices.com/blog/south-korea-digital-nomad-visa/); [Singapore embassy F-1-D page](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/sg-en/brd/m_2444/view.do?seq=761493&page=1) (not fetched – blocked).
- F-1-D does **not** permit working for Korean companies or local profit-making — [same extracts](https://www.findawayabroad.com/post/the-ultimate-list-of-freelance-visas).

**Dependents, insurance, criminal record**
- Spouse and minor children can accompany under F-1-D; marriage/birth certificates required; **each family member needs private medical insurance ≥ KRW 100M** covering treatment and repatriation — [Seattle Consulate F-1-D page (search extract)](https://www.mofa.go.kr/us-seattle-en/brd/m_23042/view.do?seq=41); [kj.nomardy.com insurance guide](https://kj.nomardy.com/korea-digital-nomad-visa-insurance/).
- Refused if sentenced to imprisonment or heavier abroad for serious crimes, or sentence ended <5 years ago; **if lived >1 year continuously in a third country within the last 5 years, a criminal-record certificate from that country is also needed** (→ for this family: likely an **Indonesian** police certificate as well as Russian) — [allvisakorea / Seattle consulate extract](https://www.allvisakorea.com/en/post/f1-visa-f-1-d-workation-digital-nomad-eligibility-requirements-and-submission-documents).

**Where to apply**
- Apply at the Korean mission **in the country where the foreigner resides**; status change inside Korea from short-term statuses **B-1, B-2, C-3** is possible if requirements are met (pilot-period rule) — [Weekly Korean NZ embassy-notice summary](https://www.weeklykoreanz.com/post/%EC%9B%8C%EC%BC%80%EC%9D%B4%EC%85%98-%EB%94%94%EC%A7%80%ED%84%B8%EB%85%B8%EB%A7%88%EB%93%9C-f-1-d-%EB%B9%84%EC%9E%90-%EC%8B%A0%EC%B2%AD-%EC%95%88%EB%82%B4); "both visa issuance (embassy) and status change (immigration office in Korea)" — [kowork.kr](https://kowork.kr/en/blog/workation-visa-korea-F-1-D).
- No source found on the Korean Embassy **Jakarta** practice for Russians on Indonesian tourist/B211 status.

**Russia–Korea entry regime 2026**
- Russians: **visa-free up to 60 days** (bilateral agreement), but **K-ETA is mandatory**; refusals of K-ETA "are not uncommon" — [Gallerix (RU travel site)](https://gallerix.org/tours/202304/kak-rossiyanam-poexat-v-yuzhnuyu-koreyu-oformlenie-k-eta-i-osobennosti-vezda/); [ketasouthkorea.com](https://www.ketasouthkorea.com/visa-requirements/russian-citizens); agreement still in force in 2026 — [VisaGo/Russiable 2026 extracts](https://russiable.com/visa-to-russia/from-south-korea/). (Secondary sources; not verified on hikorea/k-eta.go.kr.)

**Path to longer residence**
- F-1-D "is not a pathway to permanent residency"; no direct switch to F-2-7 (points-based) — would need a qualifying status first (e.g., E-7 job with a Korean employer, or independently meeting F-2-7 points: income, age, education, Korean language) — [HaniSeoul / Seoulstart / Citizen Remote search extracts](https://www.haniseoul.com/blogs/korea-f-visa-guide).

**Russia-specific obstacles**
- Western/EU banking restrictions on Russians push third-country banks to be more cautious with Russian clients for fear of secondary sanctions — [Carnegie, Dec 2025](https://carnegieendowment.org/russia-eurasia/politika/2025/12/eu-new-money-restrictions-russia). No source found specifically on Korean banks refusing Russians (GAP).

### Inferences
- **Income is not the problem** (1× GNI ≈ $38.6k vs $60–96k gross), provided they live in Busan (non-capital) and the father is ≤34 (he is 24 → valid for the whole 3–5 year horizon until age 34/35).
- **The structural problem is the freelancer status.** The father needs to present himself as the **owner of a registered overseas business with ≥1 year of history** in motion design (e.g., a company/sole-proprietorship registered outside Korea with invoices from foreign clients). A Russian ИП or a foreign LLC might be accepted, but this is unverified and embassy-dependent; the Singapore embassy explicitly rejects pure freelancers.
- **Where to apply:** Jakarta requires Indonesian residence (they are likely on a visitor-type stay in Bali → probably not accepted); a Korean mission in Russia (Moscow/Vladivostok/Irkutsk/St Petersburg) is the natural "country of nationality/residence" option; alternatively enter visa-free with K-ETA (B-1) and try a **status change in Busan** — the pilot rules allowed change from B-1/B-2/C-3.
- **Horizon:** 3 years max; 5+ years in Korea is **not achievable on F-1-D alone** — after 3 years they'd have to leave or requalify in another category. This is a major minus vs Spain's DNV (renewable, leads to long-term residence).

### Gaps
- Could not open the MOJ PDF or any embassy page (blocked) — exact 2026 wording on freelancers, own-company employment, whether dependents raise the income threshold, whether income is gross or net, and whether a re-application after 3 years is allowed.
- Jakarta embassy acceptance of non-resident applicants: no source.
- Whether a Russian ИП counts as an "overseas business": no source.

## 2. Taxes and mandatory contributions

### Takeaway
After 183 days (or earlier if the family's domicile — lease, family, life centre — is in Korea) the father is a **Korean tax resident**. The "5-of-10 years" rule taxes foreign-source income only **if paid in or remitted to Korea**, but (i) a family spending ~$7k/month in Busan remits essentially all income, and (ii) there is a real risk that income for work **physically performed in Korea** is treated as Korean-source. **Plan for full taxation: ~17–22% of gross (tax + local tax + NHIS floor), up to ~22–28% if NHIS is assessed on declared income.** No National Pension (F-1 status is excluded).

### Cited Findings
- Foreign residents who have lived in Korea ≤5 years in the last 10 are taxed on Korean-source income, and on foreign-source income **only if paid by a Korean entity or transferred to Korea** — [PwC Tax Summaries, Korea](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-korea/individual/taxes-on-personal-income).
- "Remitted" is interpreted broadly: bank transfers, **foreign card spending billed to a Korean address**, indirect remittance; "assume any offshore funds that ultimately become available for Korean spending will be deemed remitted" — [LocalNomad / fxkrw / nomadtaxguide search extracts, 2026](https://localnomad.club/en/blog/tax/korea-5-year-rule-foreign-income-tax-2026) (secondary, not NTS).
- 5-year count is cumulative within a 10-year look-back — [same](https://localnomad.club/en/blog/tax/korea-5-year-rule-foreign-income-tax-2026).
- Comprehensive income tax rates 6–45%; **6% bracket up to KRW 14M** (raised from 12M) — [NTS rate page](https://www.nts.go.kr/nts/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?mi=2227&cntntsId=7667); brackets used: 14M 6% / 50M 15% / 88M 24% / 150M 35% / 300M 38% / 500M 40% / 1B 42% / above 45% (NTS page; bracket list from my knowledge of the 2023 amendment, consistent with the extract) — plus **local income tax = 10% of income tax**.
- 19% flat rate is for **employment income from Korean employment** only, not for self-employed foreign-client income — [nomadtaxguide extract](https://nomadtaxguide.com/blog/south-korea-workcation-visa-tax-guide/).
- **NHIS:** foreigners staying >6 months are mandatorily enrolled (since 16 Jul 2019) — [NHIS English guidance](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do); F-1-D is not among the excluded statuses (A-1/A-2/A-3/G-1) — [easylaw / search extract](https://www.easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMain.laf?csmSeq=1141&ccfNo=2&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1).
- **2026 minimum premium for foreign regional subscribers: KRW 158,630/month** (140,210 health + 18,420 long-term care; +3.8% vs 152,790 in 2025); if the calculated premium is below the average, the average is charged — [NHIS foreigner premium basis](https://www.nhis.or.kr/static/html/wbma/b/wbmab0103.html); [nhisforeigner.com 2026](https://nhisforeigner.com/2026-korea-health-insurance-rate-hike/); [NHIS 2026 rate notice](https://edi.nhis.or.kr/portal/images/popup/20251204_pop01longdesc.html). Health-insurance rate 2026 used: **7.19%** of income, LTC = 13.14% of premium (from the NHIS 2026 rate notice title; exact % not visible in extract → moderate confidence).
- **National Pension:** statuses D-1, D-2, D-3, D-4, D-6, **F-1**, F-3, G-1 are excluded from mandatory NPS coverage (Russia is otherwise a reciprocity country) — [NPS foreigner page](https://www.nps.or.kr/pnsinfo/ntpsklg/getOHAF0035M0.do); [findsemusa summary](https://www.findsemusa.com/service/post/postView.do?page=1&rows=3&stype=30&qidx=636).

### Calculation (my model, KRW 1,356/USD)
Assumptions: business income = gross − 10% work expenses; basic deductions KRW 1.5M × 3 (self, non-earning spouse, child); standard tax credit KRW 70k; no child tax credit (applies from age 8); local tax 10%; NHIS either the floor (158,630×12) or income-based (net business income × 7.19% × 1.1314). Property (lease deposit) would add to income-based NHIS — ignored.

| Gross/yr | Taxable (KRW) | Income tax | Local tax | NHIS floor | **Total (tax+NHIS floor)** | NHIS on income | **Total worst case** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $60,000 | 68.7M | $7,864 | $786 | $1,404 | **$10,054 = 16.8%** | $4,393 | **$13,043 = 21.7%** |
| $72,000 | 83.4M | $10,456 | $1,046 | $1,404 | **$12,906 = 17.9%** | $5,271 | **$16,773 = 23.3%** |
| $96,000 | 112.7M | $17,640 | $1,764 | $1,404 | **$20,808 = 21.7%** | $7,028 | **$26,433 = 27.5%** |

- "Only-remittance-taxed" optimisation: remitting $36k/yr → tax ≈ $3,700; $48k → ≈ $5,800 (+ NHIS). But see inferences: this does not fit their spending.
- Net income after tax, NHIS and 10% work expenses (realistic = NHIS floor / worst): **$5k/mo → $3,662 / $3,413**; **$6k/mo → $4,325 / $4,002**; **$8k/mo → $5,466 / $4,997**.

### Inferences
- **Realistic scenario = worst case for this family**: their Busan budget (~$7.4k/mo, section 5) exceeds their net income at $5–6k, so everything they earn ends up spent in Korea → remitted → taxable. The remittance rule helps only high earners who save abroad.
- Source-rule risk: Korean practice treats employment income for work performed in Korea as domestic-source even if paid by a foreign employer; by analogy, freelance services performed in Korea may be Korean-source (then the remittance exemption doesn't apply at all). Not verified with NTS — flag for a Korean tax adviser.
- A registered Korean business (사업자등록) with simplified expense rates (단순경비율) could lower the tax for a new small business, but F-1-D forbids local business activity, so this route is doubtful.
- Russia: once non-resident in Russia (>183 days abroad), Russian 30% on Russian-source income only; Korea–Russia DTT exists (not researched here).

### Gaps
- No NTS/MOJ statement on F-1-D taxation or on the source of remote-work income.
- Whether NHIS actually assesses foreign-source income for foreigners (vs. applying the average floor) — no primary source retrieved.
- Private insurance cost for the visa (≥KRW 100M cover × 3 people) — no quote found; estimated.

## 3. International schools and English kindergartens (child 4–5)

### Takeaway
Busan has two real international options for age 4–5: **International School of Busan (ISB, IB PYP; the former Busan International Foreign School, BIFS)** — 2026/27 fees **KRW 28.2M–42.5M/yr** (lower end ≈ early years ≈ **$20.8k**) — and **Busan Foreign School (BFS, American)** — K4 **KRW 18.06M + US$6,700/yr ≈ $20.0k**, plus KRW 1.5M registration. Korean "English kindergartens" cost ~**KRW 1.5M/month (~$1,140)** but are short-day hagwons, not full-day schools.

### Cited Findings
- **ISB 2026/27 yearly fees: KRW 28,200,770 – 42,468,922** (range across grades) — [international-schools-database / ISB tuition page extract](https://www.isbusan.org/tuition-and-fees). ISB charges non-refundable enrolment, technology-development and **capital-development fees** (amounts not in extract) — [doris.school extract](https://www.doris.school/international-schools/south-korea/busan/compare).
- ISB: not-for-profit, English-medium, founded 1983, **WASC-accredited, IB PYP/MYP/DP**; address 50 Gijang-daero, **Gijang-eup, Gijang-gun** (north-east Busan, ~20–30 min from Haeundae by car) — [ISB](https://www.isbusan.org/); [Edarabia](https://www.edarabia.com/busan-international-foreign-school-busan-south-korea/). ISB's own site has a page "the-bifs-story", i.e. ISB is the renamed BIFS — [isbusan.org/the-bifs-story](https://www.isbusan.org/the-bifs-story).
- **BFS (Busan Foreign School, US curriculum, Haeundae area) 2026/27: PK3 and K4 tuition KRW 18,060,000/yr (KRW 9,310,000/semester) *and* US$6,700/yr ($3,500/semester)** — page updated 8 Feb 2026; **registration fee KRW 1,500,000**; first-year total quoted by one aggregator as KRW 37,187,441 (incl. one-time fees) — [BFS Tuition & Fees (extract)](https://busanforeignschool.org/tuition-fees/); [international-schools-database BFS 2026/27](https://www.international-schools-database.com/in/busan/busan-foreign-school/fees). ### Inferences
- Budget line for "full-day English/international kindergarten": **~$1,700/month** (BFS or ISB early years), plus first-year one-time fees (~$1.1k registration at BFS; unknown capital fee at ISB, likely several $k).
- The English-hagwon option (~$1,100–1,300/mo incl. meals/bus) is cheaper but mostly Korean children, typically finishes early afternoon — **not** an international environment; fails the family's "international environment" criterion.

## 4. Housing

### Takeaway
Busan rents are **wolse with a large deposit**. For an ~84 m² apartment in top family areas: **KRW 15M deposit + KRW 0.89M** (older/lower-tier complex, Centum) up to **KRW 100M deposit + KRW 2.5M/month** (Marine City Xi). A realistic modern 84 m² in Centum/Haeundae/Gwangalli: **~KRW 1.6–2.0M/month with KRW 30–70M deposit** (estimate). Jeonse (lump-sum) prices in Busan have been rising for 75 consecutive weeks, fastest in Haeundae.

### Cited Findings
- **Marine City Xi, 84 m² (exclusive): deposit KRW 100M, rent KRW 2.5M/month**; sale ~KRW 1.5B, jeonse ~KRW 700M (MOLIT data, 26 Jan 2026) — [aptrank / hogangnono extract](https://www.aptrank.com/apt_detail.php?aptnameuid=46129).
- **Centum Sensible (U-dong), 84.94 m²: deposit KRW 15M, rent KRW 890k/month** (Nov 2025) — [zippoom](https://zippoom.com/%EB%B6%80%EB%8F%99%EC%82%B0/%EB%B6%80%EC%82%B0-%ED%95%B4%EC%9A%B4%EB%8C%80%EA%B5%AC-%EC%9A%B0%EB%8F%99-%EC%84%BC%ED%85%80%EC%84%BC%EC%8B%9C%EB%B9%8C%EC%95%84%ED%8C%8C%ED%8A%B8/ihdvce) (84.94 is likely *supply* area ≈ "25.6 pyeong", i.e. a smaller unit).

- Haeundae listing aggregators show bands "wolse 500–700k" and "800k–1M" for apartments — [zippoom Haeundae](https://zippoom.com/%EC%A7%80%EC%97%AD%EB%B3%84-%EB%B6%80%EB%8F%99%EC%82%B0/%EB%B6%80%EC%82%B0-%ED%95%B4%EC%9A%B4%EB%8C%80%EA%B5%AC/%EC%95%84%ED%8C%8C%ED%8A%B8/%EC%A0%84%EC%9B%94%EC%84%B8) (mostly older/smaller stock).
- Market-wide shift from jeonse to wolse — [iM Securities report, Sep 2025](https://www.imfnsec.com/upload/R_E09/2025/09/[08074049]_251607.pdf).

### Inferences
- Budget: **KRW 1.8M/month rent + KRW 50M deposit (~$37k locked up)** for a modern 84 m² (34-pyeong type) in Centum / U-dong / Jwa-dong / Gwangalli; Marine City/LCT sea-view = KRW 2.5M+. Myeongji (Gangseo-gu, near Gimhae airport, new-town, cheaper) is likely ~KRW 1.2–1.5M (not verified).
- The **deposit** is the key practical hurdle for foreigners: landlords typically want KRW 10–100M; lowering the deposit raises monthly rent (conversion ~5–6%/yr — unverified). Korean apartments are rented **unfurnished** (built-in kitchen, often AC); furniture/appliances one-off ~$3–5k (estimate). Short-term furnished officetels/serviced residences exist but are pricier (not quantified).
- Foreigners can legally rent; practical needs: Alien Registration Card, Korean bank account, Korean phone, often a Korean-speaking agent; deposit protection insurance (HUG) worth considering.

### Gaps
- No 2026 official average for 84 m² wolse by district; no data on management fees (관리비), typically KRW 200–400k/month for 84 m² (estimate).

## 5. Full monthly family budget in Busan (same standard as Alicante)

### Takeaway

## 6. Brief comparison facts: city life, community, climate, connectivity

### Takeaway
Busan (~3.3M) is safe, modern, beachy and has good air by Korean standards, but it is **very Korean-monolingual** (foreigners ~2.5%), the international-family scene is small, and the only visible Russian-speaking "infrastructure" is the **Choryang/Texas Street** sailor-and-trader enclave — not the kind of community a young creative family seeks.

### Cited Findings
- **Foreigners: 83,401 at end-2023 = 2.5% of Busan's population** — [Busan city (English press)](https://www.busan.go.kr/eng/ai-translated-press-releases/1688227); foreign students reached a record **24,328** (Sep 2026) — [Korea Times, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/20260922/busans-foreign-student-population-reaches-record-high-of-24328).
- **Russian-speaking community:** Texas Street / Choryang (near Busan Station, port) — businesses with Cyrillic signs serving Russian sailors/traders and Koryo-saram; as of March 2025 "mostly Russians" there — [Wikipedia: Busan / Texas Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Street); [Ash Gallagher substack](https://ashleighgallagher.substack.com/p/busans-own-texas-town). Tripadvisor reviewers call it "Russian street"/"Russian mob" (anecdotal) — [Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowUserReviews-g297884-d16699725-r719067166-Texas_Street-Busan.html).
- **Air quality:** 2025 annual PM2.5 **14.8 µg/m³** (AQI 56 "moderate", ~3× WHO guideline) — [IQAir](https://www.iqair.com/om/newsroom/busan-among-top-10-most-polluted-cities-in-the-world-3-24-2026); Busan had the **lowest PM2.5 of the 7 Korean metropolitan cities** every year since 2020 — [Busan city press release](https://www.busan.go.kr/eng/ai-translated-press-releases/1718562); but on **24 Mar 2026 Busan was briefly in the world top-10 most polluted** (yellow-dust/fine-dust episode); high-dust season Dec–Mar — [IQAir](https://www.iqair.com/sa/newsroom/busan-among-top-10-most-polluted-cities-in-the-world-3-24-2026).

### Inferences (general knowledge, NOT verified by searches in this session — label as such)
- Climate: hot, very humid Jul–Aug (~30 °C, monsoon "jangma" late Jun–Jul), typhoon exposure Aug–Sep, dry cold-ish winters (~0–10 °C) — sunnier and milder than Seoul but clearly less pleasant than Alicante's dry Mediterranean climate.
- Connectivity: KTX Busan–Seoul ~2.5 h; Gimhae airport has mainly East/Southeast Asia routes (Japan, China, Vietnam, Thailand) and few/no long-haul flights to Europe; no direct flights to Russia were available after 2022 (unverified for 2026).
- Safety and healthcare: Korea is very safe, hospitals are excellent and affordable under NHIS; English is limited outside large hospitals' international clinics.
- Lifestyle for 24-year-olds: great beaches (Haeundae, Gwangalli), cafés, BIFF film festival, a growing design/content scene, but the creative/start-up and English-speaking scene is far smaller than Seoul; many expats describe Busan as relaxed but with a limited international social circle.

## Overall: where Busan beats / loses to Alicante (synthesis for the writer)

**Busan beats Alicante on:** ultra-modern high-rise living by the sea; safety; efficient, cheap public transport and taxis; fast internet; efficient healthcare; exciting Asian culture and proximity to Japan/SE Asia (short trips to Bali); a visa whose income bar the family already clears (1× GNI ≈ $38.6k for under-35s outside Seoul); no social-security contributions for the self-employed beyond NHIS (no NPS for F-1).

**Busan loses to Alicante on:**
1. **Legal horizon** — F-1-D max 3 years, no route to F-2/F-5; the family's 3–5+ year plan does not fit. Spain's DNV is renewable and counts toward long-term residence/citizenship.
2. **Eligibility risk** — pure freelancers are not clearly eligible; needs a ≥1-year registered overseas business; only 743 visas ever issued, consulates inexperienced; Russians face K-ETA refusals and bank caution.
3. **Cost** — ~$7.4k/month at the Alicante standard + ~$37k deposit; international kindergarten ~$20k/yr.
4. **Taxes** — realistically 17–22% (up to ~28%) of gross because all spending is remitted; no special regime for freelancers.
5. **Integration** — 2.5% foreigners, Korean-only daily life, small international-family scene, Russian community limited to a port-area enclave.
6. **Climate** — humid summers, typhoons, spring fine dust vs. Alicante's dry sunny climate.
